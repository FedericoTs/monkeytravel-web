"Free" and "no account" are not the same thing, and the travel software industry has quietly merged them.

Almost every AI trip planner marketed as free will let you type a destination, watch a loading animation, and then ask you to sign in before it shows you what it made. You have given it your intent, your dates and your budget. It has given you nothing yet.

We went through the main tools in 2026 and checked one specific thing: **can you see a real itinerary before creating an account?**

## Why they gate it

Not cynicism, economics. Understanding the reason tells you which gates will get worse.

**Booking is the business model.** Most of these tools make money when you book a hotel or a tour through them. An anonymous visitor cannot be attributed to a booking, so the account is how the tool gets paid. Mindtrip is the clearest example: the planner sits behind a Google or Apple login as of mid-2026 and the flow points toward booking inside the app.

**Generation costs real money.** Every itinerary costs the provider in model calls and places-data lookups. An account is a natural throttle.

**Retention looks better with an email.** A tool with your address can email you. One without you cannot.

None of those are dishonest. But they explain why the gate usually sits exactly where it hurts most: after you have done the work of describing your trip, before you see the result.

## The test to run, whatever you pick

Signup policy is one filter. It does not tell you whether the plan is any good. Once you have output, run this:

1. **Trace day one on a map.** Take the stops in the order given. If the route crosses the city and comes back, you were handed a list of good places, not a plan.
2. **Check three venues are open.** Pick the ones on the day you actually care about. AI tools invent opening hours with total confidence, and a Monday-closed museum on Monday is the classic failure.
3. **Count the gaps.** A day with six stops and no travel time budgeted is a day you will not complete.
4. **Try to change one thing.** Ask for day two cheaper, or a stop swapped. How hard that is tells you what the next two weeks of planning will feel like.

Most tools pass one and fail three. That is not a reason to avoid them, it is a reason to treat any first output as a draft.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there a genuinely free AI trip planner with no signup?**
Yes, though the list is short. MonkeyTravel generates a full day-by-day itinerary with no account at any point. Wonderplan and iPlan.ai are lighter on gating than most. Mindtrip and Layla both require an account before you see output.

**Why do free trip planners ask me to create an account?**
Three reasons, all economic: most make money on bookings and cannot attribute a booking to an anonymous visitor; every generated itinerary costs them real money in model and map-data calls, so the account acts as a throttle; and an email address is the only way they can bring you back.

**Is it safe to use a trip planner without an account?**
It is generally safer, not riskier. No account means no stored profile, no password to leak, and no marketing list. The trade is that nothing is saved for you automatically, so keep or export the plan yourself.

**Can I still save or share the itinerary without signing up?**
It depends on the tool. Some mint a shareable link without an account; others require registration to persist anything. Check this before you invest an hour in a plan, because losing it on a page refresh is the common failure.

**Does no-signup mean lower quality?**
No. The gate is a business decision, not a capability one. Judge the output instead: trace day one on a map, check three opening times, and see whether the day has realistic gaps between stops.

**What is the catch with free AI trip planners?**
Usually one of three: a paywall on the genuinely useful feature (Wanderlog puts route optimisation in Pro at $39.99/yr), a push toward booking through the tool, or an account requirement before you see anything. Work out which one you are accepting.
