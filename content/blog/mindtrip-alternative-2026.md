Most people looking for a Mindtrip alternative are not unhappy with the itineraries. They are unhappy with the order of operations.

As of mid-2026 Mindtrip puts a Google or Apple login in front of the planner. You hand over an account before you have seen a single day of output, and the flow is built to move you toward booking inside the app. If that trade feels backwards to you, you are the reason this page exists.

Here are six alternatives, sorted by what they are actually for rather than by a score out of ten.

## First: is Mindtrip actually wrong for you?

Worth checking before you switch, because it does two things genuinely well.

It handles **screenshots**. Hand it a saved reel or a photo from social media and it will map the places in it. Nothing else on this list does that as cleanly.

It has **real curated guides** underneath the model, so suggestions are less generic than raw AI output.

If those matter more to you than seeing output before signing up, stay. The rest of this page is for everyone else.

## 1. Wanderlog — if the research is already done

The strongest organiser of the group. You have a list of places from friends, Instagram and a spreadsheet; Wanderlog turns it into a map, a route and a structured day.

**Cost:** free tier covers pins, days, collaboration and manual budgets. **Pro is $39.99/yr** and adds offline access, route optimisation, Gmail scanning, Google Maps export and an AI assistant.

**The catch:** route optimisation is the best thing it does and it lives behind the paywall. Budget for Pro or you are using a very good notepad.

**Pick it if:** you already know where you are going and the problem is order and logistics.

## 2. Layla — if you want the trip to look good

Formerly Roam Around. Leans visual and aspirational, strong on photogenic cafés and viewpoints, and it produces itineraries that look like something you would post.

**Pick it if:** aesthetics drive your trips and you want a single paid tool that covers inspiration through booking.

**Skip it if:** you want a dense, efficient day. The visual bias is a real editorial choice and it shows up in what gets recommended.

## 3. Wonderplan — if you want free and simple

A no-frills generator. Fewer features, less polish, no real collaboration layer, but it will produce a plan without much ceremony.

**Pick it if:** you want a quick starting draft and nothing else.

## 4. ChatGPT — if you want to argue with it

Enormous flexibility. You can ask follow-up questions no purpose-built tool anticipates, and it will restructure on request.

**The catch, and it is a real one:** it invents things. Restaurants that closed, opening hours that were never true, and a confident tone throughout. It also has no map, so "these three are near each other" is a claim you have to verify yourself.

**Pick it if:** you enjoy the back-and-forth and you will fact-check the output.

## 5. iPlan.ai — if you are new to this

Deliberately simple. Fewer options, less to learn, a reasonable first itinerary. Best described as training wheels: useful at the start, and most people move on.

**Pick it if:** this is your first time letting software plan a trip.

## 6. MonkeyTravel — if you want to see it before you sign up

This is ours, so weigh it accordingly.

We made the opposite bet to Mindtrip on exactly the point that brought you here: **no account, ever.** You get a day-by-day schedule with real venues and current prices, at three budget levels, in about thirty seconds, and you can read all of it before deciding whether we deserve an email address.

The other difference is what comes out. Most of this list gives you a map and a list of places. We give you a schedule — stops grouped by day, ordered so the day is walkable, with the travel between them accounted for.

**Where we are worse:** Wanderlog beats us on collaboration and Mindtrip beats us on open-ended discovery from nothing. We also get things wrong sometimes — a venue closes, a price moves — so every stop stays editable.

**Pick it if:** you want a finished plan you can read first and edit by talking to it.

## How to actually choose

Ignore feature lists for a moment and answer one question: **is your research done?**

If yes, you want an organiser. Wanderlog, and pay for Pro.

If no, you want a generator, and the real question becomes how much you are willing to commit before seeing the output. Mindtrip and Layla want the account first. We do not.

Then run the same test on whatever you pick. Take day one and trace it on a map. If the stops zigzag across the city, you were handed a list of good places rather than a plan.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Mindtrip free?**
The core planning is free, but as of mid-2026 you need a Google or Apple account to reach the planner at all. There is no subscription gate on planning; the business model is booking through the app rather than a monthly fee.

**What is the closest alternative to Mindtrip?**
For the same generate-from-scratch experience, Wonderplan and iPlan.ai are the nearest like-for-like, both lighter on gating. If you specifically want to avoid an account, MonkeyTravel is the only one on this list that shows a full itinerary with no signup at any point.

**Is Wanderlog a Mindtrip alternative?**
Only partly. Wanderlog is an organiser, not a generator — it is strongest once you already know where you are going. If you came to Mindtrip because you did not know where to start, Wanderlog will feel like an empty notebook.

**Which alternative is best for multi-city trips?**
Wanderlog for pure route logistics if you have already chosen the cities. For deciding how to split days across cities in the first place, you want a generator that handles multi-city natively rather than one city at a time.

**Do any of these let me edit the plan by chat?**
Some do and some make you drag boxes around a canvas. It is worth testing on day one of any trial: ask for "day two cheaper" and see whether the tool changes the plan or hands you an editor.

**Will switching lose my saved Mindtrip trips?**
Yes, in practice. None of these tools import from each other. If you have significant work in Mindtrip, export or screenshot it before you switch, and treat the alternative as a fresh start.
