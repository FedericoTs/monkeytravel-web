# Layla AI Review 2026: Pricing, Features & the Best Free Alternatives

Layla is one of the most talked-about AI travel planners of 2026 — 4.6 stars on the iOS App Store and 4.7 on Google Play (as of September 2026), a homepage counter past 2.35 million trips planned, a slick interface that leans on video for destination inspiration, and, since 31 July 2026, a new owner in Expedia Group. But the question we get asked most is simpler than any feature list: *is it actually free, or do you hit a paywall the moment you want the details?*

We compared Layla's free tier and its premium plan. This is the honest version — what Layla does genuinely well, where the paywall sits, what to check before you enter a card, and the best alternatives if you'd rather not pay to see your own itinerary.

We're not affiliated with Layla and we don't earn a commission from it. Layla itself is no longer independent — Expedia Group acquired it on 31 July 2026, and a planner owned by a booking company has an interest in where your bookings land. MonkeyTravel is one of the alternatives below, and we'll tell you plainly where Layla beats us.

## Quick Verdict

Layla is a well-built tool with one of the few live-pricing integrations in this category that actually works. If you travel often and you're flexible on dates, the $49/year premium can pay for itself in fare savings alone.

But where the free tier stops has moved during 2026. Early-2026 reviews found a trip overview and a total price, with the actual **day-by-day breakdown gated** behind a three-day trial or the annual premium plan; Layla's own June-2026 copy says free now covers full itinerary generation and premium buys live pricing plus PriceLock. If you just want a full itinerary to look at and share without paying, check what Layla's free tier shows you today before you count on it.

## Where Layla Wins

**Live pricing that works.** Layla's premium tier pulls real fares from Skyscanner and Booking.com, and its PriceLock feature alerts you when prices on a saved route drop. Most tools in this category *claim* live pricing; Layla is one of the few where it holds up. If you're price-sensitive and flexible, this is the reason to consider premium.

**Inspiration-first feel.** The video-led destination discovery gives Layla a different texture from the text-heavy competition. For the dreaming phase of a trip, it's genuinely pleasant to use.

**Responsive product.** The founder reportedly handles support personally, and it shows — the tool is polished and the team iterates fast.

## Where Layla Falls Short

**The paywall has sat on the part you actually want.** A trip overview and a total price aren't a plan. Early-2026 reviews put the day-by-day schedule — the thing you open the tool for — behind the trial or the ~$49/year premium; Layla's own June-2026 copy says generation is now free and premium buys live pricing. Check which you're getting. For a category where "free AI trip planner" is the single most-searched phrase, that's a meaningful gap.

**It auto-renews.** Premium renews automatically, including after any trial Layla offers you (early-2026 reviews described a three-day one). If you're only testing it, check the current cancellation terms and set a calendar reminder to cancel before renewal.

**Language support is unclear.** Layla's App Store listing says English, while its website claims 16 languages — if you plan in Spanish, Italian, or Portuguese, check what you actually get before you rely on it.

### 1. MonkeyTravel — the free, no-paywall alternative

Where Layla gates the day-by-day, [MonkeyTravel](/trips/new) gives you the whole thing free — real venue names, estimated prices, three genuinely different budget tiers, and smart day routing — without creating an account. You can go from destination to a shareable plan in about 30 seconds.

One thing Layla doesn't do at all: MonkeyTravel has a built-in **group voting** system for when your travel companions can't agree on what to do. It also plans in **English, Spanish, Italian, and Portuguese** — Layla's language support is unclear (its App Store listing says English, its website claims 16 languages), so check before you count on it. If you're planning with friends, it's not a close call.

What Layla still wins on: live flight and hotel pricing. MonkeyTravel focuses on the itinerary, not booking — you'll open a separate tab to book. If catching a fare drop is the whole point of your search, Layla premium is the better fit.

### 2. Mindtrip — if you want to book in the same place

Mindtrip is free with no paywall and folds booking (Priceline, Viator) into the same interface, on top of a large points-of-interest database and strong maps. It asks you to create an account before you plan, but for research-plus-booking without tab-switching, it's the most complete free option. Wonderplan is free and budget-first, with clean PDF export. Reviewers often describe it as closer to a template generator than a deeply personalized planner, but for a single-destination budget trip you want fast, it does the job.
