# The 2026 Travel Planning Report: What 1.1 Million Searches Reveal

Everyone has an opinion about how travel is changing. We had data, so we looked.

This report draws on two things: **more than 1.1 million searches our pages appeared in over a three-month window in 2026**, and the **real itineraries travelers built with MonkeyTravel** in the same period. The searches tell us what people are *worried about and looking for*; the itineraries tell us what they *actually plan*. Put together, four patterns define how people plan travel in 2026 — and none of them is the one the airlines keep talking about.

## How We Read the Data

Search figures come from the queries our travel content surfaced for (via Google Search Console) — a window into demand, not a global census, but a large and honest one. Trip-shape figures come from itineraries planned on MonkeyTravel, reported as proportions and rankings rather than raw counts, because behavior patterns are what hold up. We didn't run a survey and we're not selling a forecast. This is just what the numbers say.

## Pattern 1: Planning Is Now AI-First — and Aggressively Free-First

The single loudest signal in the data is that "AI trip planner" has stopped being a novelty search and become a category. Hundreds of distinct variants — *ai trip planner*, *best ai for travel planning*, *ai itinerary generator*, *which ai is best for trip planning* — show up across the quarter, and they're growing, not fading.

But the more interesting detail is the qualifier people attach. **"Free" is the dominant modifier by a wide margin.** In our data, searches for *free ai trip planner* convert to clicks at roughly **5.9%** — more than three times the rate of the generic *ai trip planner* (about 1.7%). Travelers have decided that AI trip planning is something that should not cost money, and they search accordingly.

**What it means:** the winning products in this category won't be the ones with the flashiest paywall. They'll be the ones that give a real plan away and monetize somewhere else. For a look at how the current tools stack up, we keep an honest [comparison of the best AI trip planners](/blog/best-ai-trip-planners-2026-compared) updated.

## Pattern 2: Travelers Are Anxious About Two Things — Weather and Access

Strip out the tool searches and two anxieties dominate everything else.

**Weather timing.** One of the largest content clusters in our entire dataset is people trying to *not get rained on*. Searches like *monsoon season southeast asia*, *when is monsoon season in Thailand 2026*, *rainy season Bali*, and dozens of country-and-month variants pull in thousands of impressions a quarter. People are no longer just picking a place — they're pre-checking whether the place will be underwater when they arrive. (We built a [monsoon season guide](/blog/monsoon-season-where-to-go-and-avoid) precisely because the demand was so relentless.)

**Access.** The other giant cluster is passports and visas. A single query — *visa requirements 2026* — surfaced in over **10,000 searches** in three months, and the *Henley passport index* and its variants pulled thousands more. In a year of shifting entry rules and new travel authorizations, "can I even get in?" has become a first-order planning question, not an afterthought.

**What it means:** the modern trip decision is *when* and *whether* before it's *where*. Timing and access anxiety now sit at the very top of the funnel — and content or tools that answer them early win the traveler early.

## Pattern 3: The Default Trip Is One Week — and Increasingly Multi-City

Here's where the real itineraries are more honest than any survey. Among trips planned on MonkeyTravel:

| Trip length | Share of trips |
|---|---|
| Long weekend (1–3 nights) | ~23% |
| The "long week" (4–7 nights) | ~50% |
| Extended (8–14 nights) | ~27% |
| The average trip | **~6 nights** |

Half of all trips fall into that 4-to-7-night "long week" — the classic paid-vacation shape. But the quietly important number is this: roughly **1 in 12 trips now spans more than one city**. The single-destination holiday is no longer the default assumption; people are stitching Athens to Santorini, Tokyo to Kyoto, Dubai to Italy in one go. Multi-city planning used to be a power-user behavior. In 2026 it's mainstream, and it's the hardest thing to do by hand — which is exactly why people reach for a tool. If you're planning one, our guide to [how to plan a multi-city trip](/blog/how-to-plan-a-multi-city-trip) covers route order, open-jaw flights and the transit days most itineraries forget to budget.

**What it means:** the trip is getting more complex faster than the tools most people default to (a notes app, a spreadsheet, a chat thread). The gap between "how ambitious the trip is" and "how people plan it" is the opportunity.

## Pattern 4: Where People Are Actually Planning to Go

When we rank the most-planned destinations on MonkeyTravel, the leaders are exactly the icons you'd expect — **Paris, Tokyo, and Athens** at the top, followed by **Bali, Singapore, and Cancún** — trailed by a very long, very diverse tail of everywhere else, from Sicily to Georgia's Caucasus to Malaysia's Johor Bahru. That long tail matters: it says the "top 10 lists" undersell how idiosyncratic real trips are.

Demand is also sharply **seasonal and month-shaped**. Searches like *where to go in November 2026* and *where to travel in December 2026* spike months ahead, and the interest map rotates through the year — monsoon-avoidance in autumn, Christmas-market and Southern-Hemisphere-summer splits in December, shoulder-season Europe in spring. People plan by the calendar, not just by the map. (We publish a [month-by-month "where to go" series](/blog/where-to-go-in-september) for exactly this reason.)

## The 2026 Traveler, In One Sentence

They start planning by asking *when* and *whether* — is it dry, can I get in — reach for an AI tool they expect to be free, and build a roughly one-week, increasingly multi-stop trip to a mix of icons and idiosyncratic long-tail places. That's a very different traveler than the "book a package to one beach" customer the industry was built for.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What is the biggest travel trend in 2026?

Two, really: planning has gone AI-first (with "free" as the dominant expectation), and the decision now starts with *timing and access* — weather (monsoon/dry season) and visa/passport rules — before destination. In our data those anxiety clusters outweigh almost everything except the tool searches themselves.

### How long is the average trip in 2026?

Among itineraries planned on MonkeyTravel, the average is about **six nights**, and roughly half of all trips fall in the 4–7 night "long week" range. Long weekends make up about a quarter, and extended 8–14 night trips about another quarter.

### Are multi-city trips becoming more common?

Yes. Roughly **1 in 12** trips planned now spans more than one city — Athens-plus-the-islands, Tokyo-plus-Kyoto, and similar. Multi-city used to be a power-user behavior; it's now mainstream, and it's the single hardest trip type to plan by hand.

### What are the most-planned destinations for 2026?

Paris, Tokyo, and Athens lead, followed by Bali, Singapore, and Cancún — but the striking thing is the length and diversity of the tail. Real trips are far more idiosyncratic than any top-10 list suggests, and demand rotates sharply by month.
