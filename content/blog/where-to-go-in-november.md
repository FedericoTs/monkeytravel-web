# Where to Go in November 2026: 12 Destinations Where the Off-Season Is the Whole Point

November is the month the travel calendar forgets. It sits in the dead zone between the autumn-color rush and the Christmas-market surge, with no marquee season of its own in the Northern Hemisphere — which is exactly why it is the best-value month on the calendar. Airlines and hotels discount hard into the gap between school holidays, the Mediterranean has emptied, and the crowds that clog every headline destination in summer are simply gone. Prices haven't yet spiked for the holidays. For about three weeks, the world is quietly on sale.

But November isn't just a cheaper version of Europe. It's the pivot month of the global weather map. The northeast monsoon retreats and flips mainland Southeast Asia into its long dry season; the Middle East and North Africa cool from furnace to genuinely perfect; and below the equator it's late spring — jacarandas going purple in Buenos Aires, the wind warming up over Cape Town, fifteen-hour days returning to New Zealand. These are twelve places where November is not the consolation prize but the point, with real daily costs and the honest trade-offs.

## 1. Thailand — The Cool Dry Season Opens

November is the month Thailand exhales. The northeast monsoon pulls the rain off the Andaman coast, so Phuket, Krabi, and the Phi Phi islands dry out and the sea clears, while Bangkok settles to a merely-warm 32°C with the humidity finally broken and Chiang Mai's nights turn cool. It's also festival season: the Loy Krathong river lights and Chiang Mai's Yi Peng sky-lantern release both fall in late November this year.

**Cost reality:** $40–70/day mid-range — the islands cost more than the north, and prices climb all month as high season ramps toward December.
**The trade-off:** the Gulf islands (Koh Samui, Koh Phangan, Koh Tao) run the opposite rain calendar and are still catching the tail of their wet season in November. Go Andaman side, not Gulf side, and book festival-week beds in Chiang Mai well ahead.

## 2. Kerala & Southern India — The Post-Monsoon Payoff

The southwest monsoon has cleared by November, and Kerala is at its best: backwaters full and green, 28–31°C days with the humidity finally lifting, and the Western Ghats' tea hills around Munnar at their most walkable. This is the front edge of South India's long, comfortable dry season — temple towns, houseboats, and hill stations all in their window at once.

**Cost reality:** $30–55/day — one of the best-value regions on this entire list; a night on a backwater houseboat is the splurge and still modest.
**The trade-off:** the far southeast — the Tamil Nadu coast around Chennai — catches the *northeast* monsoon in November, so the rain simply shifts to the opposite coast. Stay on the Kerala side and in the Ghats.

## 3. Oman — The Furnace Switches Off

From November the Gulf heat breaks: Muscat settles at 28–30°C, the wadis are swimmable again, and desert nights in the Wahiba Sands turn cool enough for a fire. It's the opening of Oman's short, near-perfect window — the canyon country of Jebel Shams, the fjord-like Musandam peninsula, and long empty beaches with not a resort wall in sight.

**Cost reality:** $90–140/day — Oman isn't cheap, and a 4x4 is close to mandatory for the interior.
**The trade-off:** distances are big and public transport is thin, so budget for the rental and fuel. Travelers who want a beach and nothing else will find it very quiet — that stillness is the point, but know it going in.

## 4. Jordan (Petra & Wadi Rum) — Autumn on the Desert Trails

November is Jordan's sweet-spot shoulder: 18–22°C days that make walking Petra's full back-country loop or scrambling through Wadi Rum genuinely pleasant, without summer's heat or the cold snaps that arrive in December. The Dana-to-Petra trek and the wider Jordan Trail are in their best walking window of the year.

**Cost reality:** $80–130/day — Petra passes and guides push this up; the Jordan Pass bundles the visa and site entry and pays for itself in a couple of days.
**The trade-off:** desert nights get cold fast, and a Wadi Rum camp can drop toward freezing after dark. Pack real layers, not just a swimsuit for the Dead Sea.

## 5. Egypt — The Nile at Its Best

November opens Egypt's prime season. Luxor and Aswan hold 25–28°C — cool enough to actually explore the Valley of the Kings at midday — the Nile-cruise boats run a full schedule, and Cairo is mild. The Red Sea at Hurghada and Marsa Alam sits around 26°C and stays diveable.

**Cost reality:** $50–90/day mid-range; a multi-night Nile cruise is the big-ticket item and worth building the trip around.
**The trade-off:** this is when the season fills. Abu Simbel and the Giza plateau get busy from mid-month, and Christmas–New Year prices are already visible on the horizon, so reserve the cruise and internal flights ahead.

## 6. Marrakech, Morocco — The Souks Without the Furnace

Summer Marrakech tops 40°C; November brings it back to a walkable 22–24°C by day with cool evenings and clear light on the High Atlas peaks, already dusted with early snow. The medina, the Jardin Majorelle, and day trips out to the Ourika Valley or the Atlantic wind at Essaouira are all at their most pleasant.

**Cost reality:** $50–85/day; a riad with a courtyard is the classic mid-range stay and often lands under $70.
**The trade-off:** nights get genuinely cold and many riads heat their rooms poorly — ask about heating before you book. Early snow can also close the High Atlas passes if you're chasing the Sahara beyond the mountains.

## 7. Madeira, Portugal — The Atlantic's Evergreen Spring

While the rest of Europe reaches for a coat, Madeira holds a mild 20–22°C in the Atlantic, its *levada* trails green and walkable and its laurel forest at its most dramatic. Funchal stays lively, dolphins and whales are still offshore, and off-season short-haul flights from across Western Europe are a fraction of the summer fare.

**Cost reality:** $80–120/day — European prices, but well below peak, and the flights are where the November saving really shows.
**The trade-off:** it's an Atlantic island, so weather is changeable and the north coast catches more cloud and rain than the south. Build flexible trail days and follow the sun across the island rather than locking in one hike.

## 8. Oaxaca, Mexico — Día de Muertos

The first days of November are Oaxaca's — and Mexico's — most extraordinary moment. For Día de Muertos (October 31 to November 2), the city fills with marigold-draped altars, candlelit cemetery vigils, and *comparsas* winding through the streets. Beyond the festival it's dry-season Oaxaca: 26°C days, clear skies, mezcal country, and the mineral terraces of Hierve el Agua at their best.

**Cost reality:** $45–75/day; festival week pushes lodging up sharply, while the rest of the month is easy value.
**The trade-off:** for Día de Muertos itself, book two to three months out — Oaxaca sells out and prices spike for those specific nights. Arrive a few days after and you get the same city, quiet and cheap.

## 9. Buenos Aires & Argentina — Late Spring, Jacarandas Out

November is late spring in Argentina. Buenos Aires runs a warm 25°C with the jacarandas turning the avenues purple, the *parrillas* spilling onto the sidewalks, and the city still full — this is before the summer exodus empties it in January. It's shoulder season for Mendoza's wine country and the very front of the season for Patagonia.

**Cost reality:** $50–80/day mid-range — Argentina's economy makes prices swing, so carry cash and check the current exchange reality before you go.
**The trade-off:** Patagonia in November is early season. Some trails and *refugios* aren't fully open and the weather is volatile, so El Chaltén and Torres del Paine are more reliable from December on. November is for the cities and the wine.

## 10. Cape Town, South Africa — Late Spring Before the Peak

November is the last month before Cape Town's December price surge: 24–26°C, long dry days, Table Mountain clear, and the beaches and Winelands full but not yet packed. The Hermanus whale season is tailing off, the West Coast wildflowers are fading to green, and the peninsula's hiking is at its best.

**Cost reality:** $70–110/day; a rand-priced meal with local wine is a genuine bargain, though safaris add up separately.
**The trade-off:** the famous southeaster — the "Cape Doctor" — starts blowing in late spring and can shut the Table Mountain cableway for days at a stretch. Keep a flexible plan for the mountain and grab clear mornings when they come.

## 11. Vietnam's South & the Mekong — The Dry Season Opens

As central Vietnam catches the last of its rains, the south flips: Ho Chi Minh City, the Mekong Delta, and Phu Quoc island move into their long dry season from November — 30°C, low rain, the delta's floating markets busy, and the island's beaches clearing up.

**Cost reality:** $35–60/day — the south is superb value, with street-food dinners for a few dollars; see our [cheapest destinations in Asia guide](/blog/cheapest-destinations-in-asia) for stretching it further.
**The trade-off:** central Vietnam (Hoi An, Hue, Da Nang) is the exception — it runs its wettest weeks in October and November and can flood, so don't build the classic north-to-south loop around it this month.

## 12. New Zealand's South Island — Late Spring, Empty Trails

Flip the hemisphere. November is late spring in New Zealand — fifteen-hour days returning, snow still capping the Southern Alps, and the Great Walks (the Routeburn, the Kepler) reopening for the season without the December–January crush. Queenstown, Wanaka, and Fiordland are green, uncrowded, and cheaper than they'll be a month later.

**Cost reality:** $110–170/day — New Zealand is a long-haul splurge, but November undercuts the summer rates.
**The trade-off:** spring weather here is genuinely four-seasons-in-a-day, and some alpine passes and huts open only from mid-to-late month. Check the Department of Conservation track status before committing to any multi-day hike.

## Practical Tips for November Travel

- **This is the value month — use it.** With no Northern Hemisphere holiday anchoring it, November has the softest flight and hotel prices of the second half of the year, right before December's spike. If your dates are flexible, this is the month to spend them.
- **Follow the monsoon flip, not the brochure.** November splits Asia cleanly: Thailand's Andaman coast, Kerala, and southern Vietnam dry out, while the Thai Gulf islands, Tamil Nadu, and central Vietnam catch rain. Our [monsoon-season guide](/blog/monsoon-season-where-to-go-and-avoid) maps the whole calendar so you land on the dry side.
- **The Middle East and North Africa are at their peak now.** Egypt, Jordan, Oman, and Morocco all switch from unbearable to ideal in November — the single best window before winter cold arrives. Pack layers, because desert nights get cold fast even when the days are warm.
- **Book the festival dates, wing the rest.** Oaxaca's Día de Muertos (Nov 1–2) and Thailand's lantern festivals are the only things that sell out early this month; everything else stays open and affordable well into November.
- **Compare it against peak season and you'll never go back.** The same latitudes that cost a fortune in [August](/blog/where-to-go-in-august) are calm and cheap now — and if you can push a trip one more month, prices climb, so see how the [2026 travel calendar](/blog/2026-travel-calendar) stacks up before you lock dates.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Where is the best place to go in November 2026?

For weather-per-dollar, Thailand's Andaman coast, Kerala, and southern Vietnam are the strongest picks — all moving into dry season with prices still low. For a once-a-year experience, Oaxaca's Día de Muertos (November 1–2) and Thailand's late-November lantern festivals are hard to beat, and the Middle East (Egypt, Jordan, Oman) is at its yearly best.

### Where is it warm in November?

Cross the equator or head to the desert. Southeast Asia (Thailand, southern Vietnam, Kerala) holds 28–32°C in dry season; Egypt, Jordan, Oman, and Marrakech cool to a perfect 22–28°C; and the Southern Hemisphere is in late spring — Buenos Aires, Cape Town, and New Zealand all run 22–26°C days. Madeira splits the difference with a mild 21°C Atlantic autumn.

### Is November a cheap time to travel?

It's one of the cheapest windows of the year. Wedged between the autumn and Christmas peaks, November has soft flight and hotel prices almost everywhere in the Northern Hemisphere, and dry-season Asia (Vietnam's south at $35–60/day, Kerala at $30–55/day) delivers the best value on this list. The exception is the few nights around Oaxaca's Día de Muertos, when that city spikes. [Plan your November trip](/trips/new) around the shoulder dates and the savings compound.

### Which destinations should I avoid in November?

Anywhere still catching a monsoon: Thailand's Gulf islands (Koh Samui, Koh Phangan), central Vietnam (Hoi An, Hue, which can flood), and India's Tamil Nadu coast all see rain as the weather flips. Skip early-season Patagonia too — some trails and huts aren't fully open until December. The dry-season alternatives are the Thai Andaman coast, southern Vietnam, and Kerala.
