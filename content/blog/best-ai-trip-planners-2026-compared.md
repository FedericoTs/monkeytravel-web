# Best AI Trip Planners 2026 Compared: Honest Reviews of 8 Tools

Here's the gap that matters. Among travelers who have actually used generative AI for travel, McKinsey found 84% said it improved their experience. But YouGov, asking a much broader population, found only 30% of US travelers were comfortable using AI travel tools at all in 2025 — down from 32% a year earlier — while the 40% who said they were *not* comfortable held steady year over year.

People who get past the first attempt mostly like it. Most people never get past the first attempt. That is a product problem rather than an enthusiasm problem, and it is why which tool you start with matters more than it should.

We tested seven AI trip planners hands-on on real destinations with real constraints — different budgets, group sizes, and travel styles — and assessed an eighth, Wanderlog, from its published plans and independent reviews. This is what we found, updated for the big 2026 changes: Mindtrip now books flights and hotels inside the chat, Google added a proper day-by-day trip planner (Canvas, inside AI Mode in Search), Wanderlog caps AI help on its free plan and sells unlimited AI in Pro, and Expedia bought Layla outright.

## How We Tested

We ran each tool through four scenarios: a solo budget trip to Southeast Asia, a couple's 5-day itinerary in Portugal, a group of six planning a city break in Barcelona, and a family road trip across Italy — with one exception. Wanderlog is the newest addition to this comparison and hasn't been through those scenarios yet; its section is based on Wanderlog's published plans and pricing and on independent 2026 reviews, and says so. We evaluated each tool on itinerary quality, accuracy of venue information, real-time pricing data, booking integration, group collaboration, and language support. The 2026 feature changes described below — Mindtrip Flights and Stays, Google's Canvas planner, ChatGPT's booking apps — are drawn from the companies' own announcements and documentation rather than fresh hands-on runs.

We have no affiliate or paid relationship with any tool reviewed here, and this article contains no affiliate links. We just want to give you an honest answer to a question we get constantly: *which AI travel planner is actually worth using?*

Full disclosure: **MonkeyTravel is our own product.** We've held it to the same standard as everything else here, weaknesses included, but you should know that going in.

For a broader look at how these tools are reshaping the industry, read our piece on [How AI is Changing Travel Planning](/blog/how-ai-is-changing-travel-planning).

## 1. MonkeyTravel

**What it does:** MonkeyTravel generates day-by-day itineraries with real venue names, estimated prices, and smart routing — all without requiring you to create an account. You pick a destination, set your trip length, choose from three budget tiers (Budget, Balanced, or Premium), and get a structured plan in seconds. It supports English, Spanish, Italian, and Portuguese, and has a group planning feature with a voting system for when your travel companions can't agree.

**New in 2026 — the live trip.** Most planners stop the moment the plan is done. MonkeyTravel now stays with you *on* the trip: once your dates start, it opens on **Today** — the current day's plan, front and centre. Share the trip and everyone travelling with you sees that same live view, with four one-tap controls: mark something done, skip it, flag that you're running late (which shifts the rest of the day), or swap an activity for a nearby alternative. Alongside it sits a packing checklist, a running expense split, and a feed showing who changed what. Companions don't just watch — they can act, and they don't need an account to do it. It's the plan that travels with you, not just the one you make beforehand.

**Strengths:** No signup barrier is a real differentiator — you can try it in 30 seconds. The three budget tiers produce meaningfully different itineraries, not just the same plan with different restaurant price ranges. Group voting genuinely solves a pain point that most tools ignore, and the live-trip view extends that to the days you're actually away — nothing else on this list gives your companions a shared live day view they can act on without an account. The whole product runs in Spanish, Italian and Portuguese, not just the marketing page (though Mindtrip, Wanderlog and iPlan.ai offer those languages too, so that alone isn't the reason to pick us). For a [free AI trip planner](/trips/new), it covers a lot of ground.

**Weaknesses:** No real-time pricing data. The venue information is AI-generated and should be verified before you book — opening hours and prices won't always be current. There's no flight or hotel booking integration, so you'll still need to open other tabs for that. The tool works best for itinerary structure and staying organised on the trip; it's not a replacement for a booking platform.

**Best for:** Travelers who want a solid day-by-day plan fast without creating an account, and groups that need to vote on options beforehand and stay coordinated once they're actually travelling.

## 2. Mindtrip

**What it does:** Mindtrip is one of the most fully-featured AI travel tools available in 2026. Mindtrip says its database covers over 11 million points of interest, and you research destinations and build itineraries through conversational AI. It moved hard into booking this year: **Mindtrip Flights** launched in **May 2026** — described as travel's first all-in-one agentic AI flight booking experience, built on a partnership with **Sabre and PayPal** — and **Mindtrip Stays** followed in **July 2026**, rebuilding hotel search around conversation instead of filter panels. You can now go from question to booked flight or room inside the chat.

**Strengths:** The POI database is deep, and the maps integration means you can see your itinerary geographically, which most competitors still get wrong. With Flights and Stays, it's the closest thing here to "plan me a week in Japan" ending in an actual booking without leaving the conversation. Mindtrip was named to Fast Company's World's Most Innovative Companies list for 2025, and collaborative planning lets you share and refine trips with others. It's free to use, with reservations handled by third-party partners. It's also **more multilingual than most people assume** — the app lists eight languages, including Spanish, Italian and Portuguese.

**Weaknesses:** The booking-first design is the trade-off: the product is free and the flow leads toward a reservation, which is worth knowing when you just want a plan. It's also a fast-moving product, so older reviews age badly — it launched a new, standalone conversational hotel search in July 2026, so complaints you'll find in write-ups from earlier in the year may no longer describe the tool you get today.

**Best for:** Travelers who want to research, plan, and now book flights, hotels and activities without switching between four different apps. (More detail — and the best alternatives if you'd rather not create an account — in our [Mindtrip review](/blog/mindtrip-review-2026).)

## 3. Layla AI

**What it does:** Layla is a dedicated AI travel assistant. Its own homepage counter passed **2.35 million trips planned** as of 7 September 2026, and as of the same date it rates **4.6 on the iOS App Store** and **4.7 on Google Play**. Premium runs **$49.99/year or $9.99/month** on iOS (Layla's own website quotes $49/year), unlocking live pricing pulled from Skyscanner, Booking.com and GetYourGuide, plus a PriceLock algorithm that tracks fares and pings you when a route drops. It also uses video content for destination inspiration, which gives it a different feel from text-heavy competitors. (For the full breakdown, see our [Layla AI review](/blog/layla-ai-review-2026).)

**One thing to know up front:** Layla is no longer independent. **Expedia Group acquired it on 31 July 2026.** That doesn't make the tool worse, but in a comparison piece it's worth stating plainly — a planner owned by an OTA has an interest in where your booking lands.

**Strengths:** The live pricing integration is one of the few things in this category that actually works as advertised. If you're price-sensitive and flexible on dates, PriceLock can surface real savings. This is a genuinely [well-built AI itinerary generator](/ai-itinerary-generator) that goes beyond just day-by-day plans.

**Weaknesses:** Premium auto-renews, and if Layla offers you a trial (early-2026 reviews described a three-day one), set a reminder to cancel before it converts. Be aware that where the free/paid line sits has been moving: reviews from early 2026 describe a free tier limited to a trip overview and total price, while Layla's own mid-2026 copy says free covers full itinerary generation and only live pricing and PriceLock are paid. Check what you actually get before you subscribe.

**Best for:** Budget-conscious travelers who are flexible on timing and want to catch price drops. Solo travelers or couples who want live pricing without a subscription to a full OTA.

## 4. Wanderlog

**What it does:** Wanderlog is a polished, collaboration-first trip planner best known for putting your **itinerary and a map in one view**. The free plan covers a lot of the core workflow — building an itinerary, live collaboration with travel companions, importing reservations, recommendations, and budgeting. Its AI assistant is capped on the free plan — a hands-on review in February 2026 counted five AI messages per trip — and **unlimited AI assistance is a Pro feature**. Pro starts at $39.99/year on Wanderlog's own pricing page (read September 2026) and also adds offline access, automatic Gmail scanning for bookings, Google Maps export, route optimization, and flight and car-rental deals.

**Strengths:** The itinerary-plus-map layout is one of the best in the category, and the free collaboration features are genuinely useful for a group building a trip together. Importing existing bookings and the budgeting tools make it a solid organiser even before you touch the AI.

**Weaknesses:** The thing to know: **unlimited AI help is a paid feature**, so "free AI planning" isn't quite what you get on the free tier — you get a strong free manual planner with a capped assistant, and Pro removes the cap. It doesn't book flights in-app, but it does compare live hotel prices from partners such as Booking.com and Expedia, and Pro adds flight and car-rental deals. Pricing can vary by region and promotion, so check Wanderlog's own Pro page for your rate.

**Best for:** Travelers who want a strong free collaborative planner with a map, and don't mind that unlimited AI help is a paid upgrade. (We line it up against the strongest free option in our [Wanderlog alternative](/blog/wanderlog-alternative-2026) guide.)

*A note on method: unlike the other tools here, we haven't yet run Wanderlog through our four test scenarios. This assessment is based on Wanderlog's own published plans and pricing and on independent 2026 reviews.*

## 5. Wonderplan

**What it does:** Wonderplan focuses on budget-conscious itinerary generation. You fill in a short form — destination, dates, a few preferences — and it returns a day-by-day breakdown you can reorder, add to, or remove from, then download as a PDF. It's a simple tool that does what it promises without much friction, and Wonderplan's own FAQ confirms it's free.

**Strengths:** The budget-first approach is clear and consistent — it doesn't bury cost information. PDF export is a practical feature for travelers who prefer printed itineraries or offline access. And it really is free: independent testing in 2026 found no paid tiers at all.

**Weaknesses:** It's a form, not a conversation. There's no chat box or free-text prompt, so "refining" your plan means editing the output by hand rather than asking for changes — a real difference from the tools above if you like working conversationally. It doesn't offer flight or car-rental booking — the site never mentions either, and independent testing found the "find a place to stay" button is just a plain link to Booking.com's homepage — and in testing it planned one city at a time.

**Best for:** Single-destination, budget-focused trips where you want a quick structured plan without complexity. (See our [Wonderplan review](/blog/wonderplan-review-2026) for a closer look and stronger free alternatives.)

## 6. Google Gemini

**What it does:** Google Gemini isn't a dedicated travel tool, but it has something no other entry on this list has: native access to Google Maps, Google Flights, Google Hotels, and your Gmail travel confirmations. And Google has closed its biggest gap: the **Canvas trip planner inside AI Mode in Google Search** (launched to US Labs users in November 2025 and widened through 2026) turns that data into an editable, day-by-day itinerary — daily plans, restaurants, attractions — in a side panel, rather than the wall of paragraphs Gemini used to hand back. It's US-only as we write this. In the Gemini app, custom Gems you can set up as a trip planner are free for everyone.

**Strengths:** The data access is unmatched. When Gemini says a restaurant opens at 11am, it's pulling that from Google Maps, not guessing. Real-time flight pricing, real hotel reviews, and integrated maps are the best combination of live data in any AI assistant — and with Canvas (in the US) you now get a structured itinerary on top of it, for free, if you already live in the Google ecosystem.

**Weaknesses:** It needs a Google account and works best if you're already all-in on Google — the itinerary lives in AI Mode's Canvas, which Google says you can save and share, but it isn't a collaborative trip page, and outside the US you don't get Canvas at all. Hotel booking inside AI Mode began rolling out in the US in August 2026; elsewhere booking still happens separately. There are no group features such as voting, so coordinating a trip with other people isn't its strength.

**Best for:** Travelers who want data accuracy and a free structured itinerary, are already deep in the Google ecosystem — and, for Canvas, are in the US. If you want something account-free, shareable with a group, or multilingual out of the box, a dedicated tool still fits better.

## 7. ChatGPT

**What it does:** ChatGPT is the tool most people start with, because they already have it. For travel, it's genuinely useful for brainstorming destinations, researching local customs, building rough itineraries, and drafting packing lists. There's a usable free tier; paid plans now start at **$8/month (ChatGPT Go)**, with **Plus at $20/month** for higher limits. The Expedia and Booking.com apps inside ChatGPT, which return live prices, are on the free tier too — but only outside the EU, UK and Switzerland at the time of writing.

**Strengths:** If you know how to prompt it well, ChatGPT can produce detailed travel research faster than any manual search. It's particularly good for nuanced questions — "best neighborhood to stay in Lisbon if I have young kids and want good restaurant access" — where Google returns generic listicles. For the planning and ideation phase, it's hard to beat.

**Weaknesses:** No live pricing unless the Expedia and Booking.com apps are available where you are. Only a basic map view on local searches. No dedicated travel UI. And like all general LLMs, the hallucination risk is real — it can confidently recommend places that have since closed, misjudge walking distances, or surface outdated visa information. Always verify specifics against official sources. We go deeper on this in [ChatGPT vs AI Trip Planners](/blog/chatgpt-vs-ai-trip-planners), and on how to check any generated plan before you travel in [Can You Trust an AI Travel Itinerary?](/blog/can-you-trust-ai-travel-itinerary). See also [AI Trip Planner vs Travel Agent](/blog/ai-trip-planner-vs-travel-agent).

**Best for:** The research and ideation phase of planning, not the final itinerary. Works best as a complement to a dedicated tool, not a replacement.

## 8. iPlan.ai

**What it does:** iPlan.ai is a mobile-first AI itinerary generator designed for speed. Drop in a destination, set your preferences, get a plan. It's built for the traveler who makes decisions on their phone and wants something usable in under two minutes. Note that despite often being listed as a free tool, **it is free to download but the AI itinerary builder is an in-app purchase, starting around $3.99**, with a Pro membership at $9.99.

**Strengths:** The mobile experience is genuinely optimized — most competitors feel like desktop apps crammed into a small screen. Customization is easy and the interface is clean. If you primarily plan from your phone, this is one of the more frictionless options.

**Weaknesses:** It's mobile-only in the strictest sense — there is no web planner at all, just a marketing site pointing at the two app stores. That creates friction when you want to copy details to a shared doc, book on a larger screen, or do deeper research. It's also narrower in scope than most competitors — no budget tiers, and lighter group and booking features (you can share and co-edit a plan and book tours in-app, but there's no voting and no flight or hotel booking) — and the paywall on generation puts it behind the genuinely free tools here.

**Best for:** Solo travelers who plan exclusively on mobile and need a quick, clean itinerary without complexity.

## The Verdict: Which Tool for Which Traveler

**You need a quick, free day-by-day plan:** MonkeyTravel or Wonderplan. No signup, no commitment, useful output in 60 seconds — and MonkeyTravel then stays with you as a live companion once the trip starts.

**You want to research AND book in one place:** Mindtrip. With in-chat flights, hotels and activities, it's the most complete end-to-end tool currently available.

**You're chasing the best price and flexible on dates:** Layla AI. The live pricing and PriceLock feature are worth the premium tier if you travel frequently.

**You want the most accurate real-time data:** Google Gemini. Unbeatable data access, and Canvas now gives you a real itinerary too — as long as you're happy inside the Google ecosystem.

**You want a free collaborative planner with a map:** Wanderlog, if you don't mind that unlimited AI help is a paid upgrade. For free AI planning with a group, MonkeyTravel's voting is the closer fit.

**You're planning a group trip:** MonkeyTravel's voting plus the shared live-trip view, or Mindtrip's collaborative planning. Most tools in this space still ignore the group use case. For more on group planning, see our [group trip planner](/group-trip-planner) guide.

**You're in the early brainstorming phase:** ChatGPT. Best for ideation and research; not your final planning tool.

**You live on your phone:** iPlan.ai. Optimized for mobile in a way others aren't — just budget for the in-app purchase, because generation isn't free.

No single tool does everything well. The best approach in 2026 is still layered: ChatGPT or Gemini for initial research, a dedicated tool like MonkeyTravel or Mindtrip for itinerary structure, and your OTA of choice — or Mindtrip's in-chat booking — for the actual reservations.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best free AI trip planner in 2026?

It depends on what you need. MonkeyTravel, Mindtrip, and Wonderplan are all free with no meaningful paywalls on core itinerary features. Mindtrip has the deepest data and now books flights, hotels and activities in-chat. MonkeyTravel is the fastest to get a day-by-day plan without signing up — and the only one here with a shared live day view your companions can update without an account. Wonderplan works well for single-destination budget trips. Two caveats: Wanderlog's free plan is strong for manual, collaborative planning, but **AI assistance is capped on the free tier and unlimited only in paid Pro**; and iPlan.ai is free to download while its **itinerary generation is an in-app purchase**.

### Can AI trip planners replace travel agents?

For independent travelers planning standard trips, AI planners handle the itinerary and research phase better than most travel agents at the same price point (free). Where agents still win: complex multi-destination itineraries, last-minute problem-solving, relationships with luxury properties, and situations where something goes wrong mid-trip. We covered this in detail in [AI Trip Planner vs Travel Agent](/blog/ai-trip-planner-vs-travel-agent).

### Do any AI trip planners show live flight and hotel prices?

Yes. Layla AI pulls live pricing from Skyscanner, Booking.com and GetYourGuide. Mindtrip integrates flights (via Sabre and PayPal), hotels and activities for in-chat booking. Google Gemini surfaces Google Flights and Google Hotels data. ChatGPT shows live prices only through its Expedia and Booking.com apps, which aren't available in the EU or UK yet. MonkeyTravel, Wonderplan and iPlan.ai do not currently provide live pricing; Wanderlog shows partner hotel prices but no live flight pricing on the free plan.

### Are AI-generated itineraries accurate?

It varies significantly by tool. Tools with live data connections (Gemini, Mindtrip, Layla) are more reliable on current information. Tools without a live data connection (Wonderplan, iPlan.ai — and MonkeyTravel) carry more hallucination risk on specifics like opening hours, prices, and availability. Always verify venue details before showing up.

### Which AI trip planner is best for groups?

MonkeyTravel has a group planning feature with a built-in voting system, plus a shared live-trip view so everyone follows the same day-by-day plan while you're away. Mindtrip has collaborative planning that lets multiple people edit and share an itinerary, and Wanderlog offers free live collaboration on the planning side. Most other tools on this list are built primarily for solo or couple planning.
