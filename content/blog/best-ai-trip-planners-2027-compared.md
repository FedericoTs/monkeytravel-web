# Best AI Trip Planners 2027 Compared: Honest Reviews of 11 Tools

Nearly three in ten Americans traveling this holiday season plan to use generative AI to find or plan their trip: 29%, up from 24% last year and 8% in 2023, according to Deloitte's 2026 holiday travel survey. Trust is moving more slowly. In a YouGov survey for Expedia Group in the US, UK and India, 66% said they wouldn't trust AI to book anything for them, and among the seasoned international travelers Global Rescue polls, nearly six in ten trust AI travel information only after checking it somewhere else.

That habit is the right one, and it's the lens for this comparison. Most AI trip planners start with a language model writing from what it learned, and what it learned goes stale: museums close on Mondays, restaurants shut for good, the last cable car leaves earlier than the model remembers. So the question for 2027 isn't which planner writes the most convincing day. It's which one checks that day for you.

We compared 11 tools as they stand in late November 2026: six dedicated planners, including our own, four general assistants and Tripadvisor's AI. [Our 2026 edition](/blog/best-ai-trip-planners-2026-compared) has the picture from earlier this year.

## What changed in 2026

- **Mindtrip** went from planning to booking: flights, hotels, restaurants and things to do, plus an assistant that keeps watching prices after you close the chat.
- **Layla** was bought by Expedia Group in July, stopped selling Premium to new subscribers in August, and its iPhone app now books trips itself.
- **Trip Planner AI** (tripplanner.ai) is now run by Layla's company and sends you to Layla to plan.
- **Google** brought itineraries into Maps (Ask Maps, rolling out to 30 European countries since 16 November); the Canvas planner in AI Mode is still US-only.
- **ChatGPT** retired group chats and ChatGPT agent, and added restaurant reservations.
- **Microsoft Copilot** retired its group chats and Copilot Mode in Edge.
- **Stippl**, a group-trip app new to this comparison, can now edit your trip from its AI chat and book hotels in the app.
- **iPlan.ai** is out: its iPhone app hasn't been updated since November 2025.
- **MonkeyTravel**, ours, now checks each plan's closing days and holidays after writing it.

## How we built this comparison

Every fact about another company's product comes from that company's own pages (pricing, help center, terms, press releases, app-store listings) or from a dated independent test, all read on 26 November 2026. We didn't re-run our 2026 hands-on scenarios: too much changed this year for old runs to be fair. Where a vendor's pages contradict each other, we say so; where we couldn't confirm something, we left it out.

We have no affiliate or paid relationship with any tool here, and this article has no affiliate links. Full disclosure: **MonkeyTravel is our own product.** Everything we say about it was checked against how it actually works, weaknesses included.

## 1. MonkeyTravel

**What it does:** Give it a destination and dates, pick a budget level (Budget, Balanced or Premium), a pace, who's going (solo, a couple, a family or a group), any diet and up to ten must-dos, and in about 30 seconds you get a day-by-day plan with named places, times and cost estimates. The site and the plans themselves work in English, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese. Type a whole country or region for five days or more ("Thailand, 14 days") and it offers a route through several bases instead, up to five cities and 21 days (more in our [multi-city trip planner](/multi-city-trip-planner)).

**New for 2027: checks after the plan.** The plan is still written by an AI model (Google's Gemini), and models get closing days wrong. So once it's written, MonkeyTravel puts a second, narrow question to the model: on which weekdays each sight, market, garden and shop in it is shut, and whether it's shut on the trip's big holidays (Christmas to Epiphany, 1 May, Western and Orthodox Easter, US Thanksgiving). For about 160 of the sights plans include most, from the Louvre and the Vatican Museums to the Met, a list we checked against official pages decides instead, and for most of them supplies the opening hours too. The days are then reordered so sights fall on open days; a sight shut for the whole trip is swapped; a visit to a listed sight that would run past closing is moved within its day or cut short; places closed for long renovations, like the Centre Pompidou until 2030, are kept out. Must-dos the first draft forgot go back in where they fit.

**Once you save it** (free account): the trip's places to eat are looked up on Google Maps, and any that Maps lists as closed is replaced or removed. Emails follow the trip, from the forecast and a packing list two weeks out to an email each evening of the trip with the next day's plan and its route in Google Maps. During the trip it opens on **Today**, and once you share it, the people traveling with you can tap *Running late*, *Skip this*, *Swap nearby* or *Done for today* without making an account, and anyone with the link can vote on activities. You can also invite editors.

**Without an account:** up to five plans a day, an assistant that answers questions and changes a day, a private link that keeps the plan for 30 days, and an email with the plan. Tick "remind me" and you also get an email in the week before the trip and another the day before. A free account raises the limit to 30 AI-planned trips a month. There is no paid plan.

**Strengths:** You see a full plan before giving anyone your email. The checks go after the mistake this article keeps coming back to, the museum on its closing day or the sight shut over Christmas, before you find it at a locked door. Group features work for people who won't sign up for anything. [Try it free](/trips/new).

**Weaknesses:** No booking inside: links open Google Flights, Booking.com and Airbnb searches for your dates. No live prices: costs are AI estimates. The checks have edges: hours are checked only for the listed sights, other sights' closing days come from a second AI answer that can also be wrong, and places to eat are looked up only once you save. It's a website, with no app in the stores, and four languages is fewer than most dedicated planners here.

**Best for:** A day-by-day plan you can actually walk, fast and without an account, especially over holidays, when closures bite; and groups who want to vote on the plan and follow the same day once there (see our [group trip planner](/group-trip-planner)).

## 2. Mindtrip

**What it does:** A conversational planner built on a large travel database (over 11 million points of interest, by its own count) and, per its FAQ, on OpenAI for the chat. In 2026 it became a booking tool: **Flights** launched in May with Sabre and PayPal, **Stays** in July with hotel inventory from Priceline, Expedia, Booking.com, Agoda and others, and its FAQ now lists flights, places to stay, restaurants and things to do. A **Personal Travel Assistant** (30 September) remembers your preferences between trips, watches hotel prices, tries other dates for a better flight and prepares briefings and packing recommendations.

**Price, platforms, languages:** Free for travelers; the paid product, Mindtrip for Business, is for tourism boards and hotels. It's on the web and iPhone (its FAQ mentions no Android app), and the iPhone app needs iOS 26 or later. The app is in eight languages, including Spanish, Italian and Portuguese, with voice chat in all eight since November. 4.7 on the US App Store (888 ratings).

**Groups:** Invite friends or family to plan with you, in a group chat with recaps of what you missed. We found no voting.

**Accuracy:** It draws on partners' data (Tripadvisor, Viator, Priceline, Agoda and Google among them) and added an opening-hours filter in November. Its terms still warn that AI output may be inaccurate, incomplete or out of date.

**Strengths:** The most complete research-to-booking tool here: from "a long weekend in Lisbon" to a booked flight and room without leaving the conversation.

**Weaknesses:** The free product leads toward a reservation, which matters if you only want a plan. No Android app. It changes every few months, so older reviews, ours included, age fast.

**Best for:** Researching, planning and booking in one place. (Our [Mindtrip review](/blog/mindtrip-review-2026) and [Mindtrip alternatives](/blog/mindtrip-alternative-2026) go further.)

## 3. Wanderlog

**What it does:** A collaboration-first planner that puts the itinerary and a map in one view. You add places, each with its rating, address and opening hours, and everyone you invite edits in real time, Google Docs-style. Its AI assistant is powered by ChatGPT: it answers questions, drafts an itinerary and drops places straight into your trip. A late-November update lets you start a trip by pasting a link or sharing a reel.

**Price and limits:** Free, with an optional Pro. The free plan caps the AI (a February 2026 hands-on test counted five messages per trip before a paywall); Pro removes the cap and, per its App Store listing, adds export to Google Maps and Gmail import. The US App Store lists Pro at several prices, yearly from $31.99 to $59.99 or monthly at $5.99 and $16.99, so check what you're offered.

**Platforms and languages:** Web, iPhone, Android and a browser extension. The website is in 30 languages; the iPhone app is in English. 4.9 on the US App Store (37,000 ratings).

**Booking:** No flights, but it compares hotel prices from partners such as Booking.com and Expedia.

**Accuracy:** Opening hours on every place help when you plan by hand. The AI is another story: in that February test it first called Hagia Sophia a museum, then said entry was free (the tester notes it has been a mosque since 2020 and visitors pay), and told a family to buy €18 Colosseum tickets for a free-entry first Sunday. It corrected itself when challenged.

**Best for:** Groups who build a trip by hand, together, on a map, and don't mind paying for unlimited AI. (See [Wanderlog vs Mindtrip](/blog/wanderlog-vs-mindtrip-2026).)

## 4. Stippl

**What it does:** Stippl calls itself a free AI travel planner for the people you travel with. Around a route on a map you get day plans, a shared budget that works out who owes what, packing lists, a journal and an automatic video of the trip. Its AI writes a whole itinerary or a day's plan; since 28 October its chat can change the trip (add a night, add or remove a stop) with an undo on every change, and since 8 November it suggests places nearby and checks the forecast up to 14 days ahead.

**Price and limits:** Trips and co-planners are unlimited for free. Each account gets two AI-made itineraries, and the chat runs on a monthly allowance of AI credits. Pro makes AI trips and day plans unlimited and adds route optimisation, forwarding booking emails, calendar sync and PDF export. Stippl says the price depends on your country and where you subscribe; its own comparison page quotes €39.99 a year.

**Platforms, languages, booking:** iPhone, Android and web, in seven languages including Spanish, Italian and Portuguese; 4.6 on the US App Store (490 ratings). You can book a hotel for each stop and buy an eSIM in the app.

**Accuracy:** We couldn't find where its place data comes from or whether it checks opening hours, nor an independent test of its AI.

**Best for:** Friends planning a multi-stop trip who want the budget, packing and memories in the same app. The catch: two free AI itineraries is a tight allowance if AI is what you came for.

## 5. Layla (and what happened to Trip Planner AI)

**What it does:** Layla plans a trip in a chat. On the website, a human travel advisor then checks it, books it and supports you on WhatsApp. Since a 6 November update the iPhone app lets you book the whole trip yourself (flights, hotels, activities, car hire), and you can call an advisor from the chat.

**Ownership:** Expedia Group bought Layla on 31 July 2026. A planner owned by an online travel agency has an interest in where your booking lands; worth knowing in a comparison.

**Price and limits:** Planning is free; you pay for what you book. Layla's terms say Premium hasn't been offered to new subscribers since August, though the US App Store still lists it at $49.99 a year or $9.99 a month. In an independent test on 4 and 5 November, the website built a six-day plan without a sign-in, the iPhone app hid it until the tester made an account, and neither showed a paywall.

**Platforms and languages:** Web, iPhone and Android; the iPhone app is in English. 4.6 on the US App Store (221 ratings).

**Accuracy:** That November test caught a classic: on 26 December the plan opened with London's Natural History Museum, which closes from 24 to 26 December. It also added flights the family had already booked.

**Trip Planner AI:** tripplanner.ai is now run by Layla AI GmbH; its terms say it only refers people to Layla and booking partners.

**Best for:** An AI draft followed by a person who books and stands behind the whole trip. Just know most reviews describe a Layla that changed several times this year. (Background in our [Layla AI review](/blog/layla-ai-review-2026).)

## 6. Wonderplan

A short form (destination, dates, budget, who's traveling, interests) returns a day-by-day plan you can reorder and trim, then download as a PDF; there's no chat. It's free, though its FAQ hedges that this might not last, and a pop-up asks you to log in for full functionality. Web only, English only, and its terms say it isn't a travel agency: attraction pages send "Book Now" clicks to Viator. An April 2026 hands-on test found that adding or replacing places didn't work and a family plan included unsuitable picks. **Best for:** a quick, free, single-city plan from a form. (More in our [Wonderplan review](/blog/wonderplan-review-2026).)

## 7. Google Gemini (with AI Mode and Maps)

Google's trip planning now lives in three places, and they don't all work everywhere.

**The Gemini app** is free with a Google account. It pulls ratings, addresses, opening hours and directions from Google Maps, shows them on a map you can export to a Maps list, and uses Google Flights and Hotels data. With Personal Intelligence on it reads the bookings in your Gmail, and with Viator connected it can book tours. By Google's own help page, it can't book a flight or a hotel. The maps features need you to be 18+, signed in to a personal account with Keep Activity on. Paid plans in the US: AI Plus $4.99, AI Pro $19.99, Ultra from $99.99 a month.

**AI Mode in Search** has **Canvas**, an editable, shareable day-by-day trip plan, in English in the US only. Since August it also books select US hotels with Google Pay, and it can reserve restaurant tables in select markets.

**Google Maps** got **Ask Maps** itineraries on 16 November: day trips and multi-day plans with numbered pins, whether each stop is open, and sharing or export to Google Docs. It's on Android and iPhone and rolling out to 30 European countries, including Spain, Germany and the UK.

**Strengths:** Live data. When Gemini or Ask Maps gives you a place's hours, it's reading Google Maps rather than remembering them, the biggest accuracy advantage a general assistant has.

**Weaknesses:** The best pieces are US-first or need a Google account with activity saved; there are no group features beyond sharing; and Google's own help page warns that Gemini can present wrong information as fact.

**Best for:** Anyone already in Google's apps who wants live data on hours, flights and hotels.

## 8. ChatGPT

**What it does:** The tool most people start with. Good for brainstorming, nuanced questions ("which Lisbon neighborhood with small kids?") and rough itineraries, with web search and citations, and a map on local results in the phone apps. Since August it finds restaurant tables through OpenTable (worldwide), Resy (US) and Yelp (US and Canada).

**What changed:** Group chats were retired in July and ChatGPT agent is gone. The directory where travel apps like Expedia's and Booking.com's launched in 2025 became a Plugin Directory; those apps launched without the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, and OpenAI now only says availability depends on app, plan and region.

**Price and platforms:** Free with limits; in the US, Go is $8 a month, Plus $20, and Pro $100, $200 or $500. Web, desktop, iPhone and Android, in 59 languages.

**Accuracy:** OpenAI warns that ChatGPT can sound confident while wrong, and the travel cases are memorable. The BBC reported a couple stranded on Japan's Mount Misen after ChatGPT gave them a 5:30 p.m. last cable car that had already stopped; a Euronews writer's ChatGPT weekend in Tallinn sent her to restaurants that had closed for good; and in a Which? test it called travel insurance mandatory for Schengen visits, which Which? says it isn't unless you need a visa.

**Best for:** Brainstorming and questions, followed by a planner that checks. (More in [ChatGPT vs AI trip planners](/blog/chatgpt-vs-ai-trip-planners).)

## 9. Microsoft Copilot

Copilot will draft an itinerary on the web, in Windows, on your phone and in Edge, in 38 languages for personal accounts. But most of its travel experiments are gone: Copilot Mode in Edge was retired in May, the Browse with Copilot preview that replaced Copilot Actions has ended, and Group Chat has been retiring since 18 August. It's free, subject to capacity; Microsoft 365 Personal ($9.99 a month) or Premium ($19.99) raises the limits. Microsoft's transparency note says it can make mistakes, and in Which?'s test it said a cancelled or delayed flight always means a full refund, which Which? says isn't the case. Copilot's personal and work apps are merging, and chats not moved over by 31 December 2026 will be deleted. **Best for:** a quick first draft if you already live in Microsoft 365.

## 10. Perplexity

An answer engine built around sources: every answer carries citations, which makes it the easiest assistant here to fact-check, and it scored highest (71 out of 100) in Which?'s test of six AI tools. In 2025 it added hotel booking in its phone apps and OpenTable reservations; US Pro subscribers get hotel discounts capped at $200 a booking. Free gives you three Pro searches a day; Pro is $20 a month or $200 a year. It's a research tool, not a planner: no trip view, no day-by-day plan to edit, no group features, and its terms still warn of errors. **Best for:** checking the details of a plan you already have.

## 11. Tripadvisor

The free Tripadvisor app builds an itinerary from other travelers' recommendations, summarizes reviews with AI and books from over 400,000 experiences and 600,000 hotels (28 languages; 4.8 from 482,000 ratings on the US App Store). Since April its hotels and Viator's experiences are also available inside Anthropic's Claude, where you click through to book. The risk is the summaries: in July 2026 Which? found them playing down serious problems, including a Cape Verde hotel whose summary praised its cleanliness despite 102 mentions of food poisoning in its reviews, and Ollie, Tripadvisor's trip-planning chatbot, called food poisoning there unlikely. Tripadvisor disagreed with the premise and called the chatbot a product in development. **Best for:** reading what other travelers said, in the reviews themselves.

## Accuracy: who checks what

This is where the tools differ most and the marketing says least. What each one checks, from its own documentation, and what independent testers documented in 2025 and 2026:

| Tool | Live place data? | What it checks for you | Known limits or slip-ups |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MonkeyTravel** | Not while writing; Google Maps for places to eat on saved trips | Closing days and big holidays for sights; hours for its listed sights; long closures; closed places to eat | Hours aren't checked beyond the list; costs are estimates |
| **Mindtrip** | Partner data (Tripadvisor, Google and others) | Opening-hours filter when exploring | Terms warn AI output can be out of date |
| **Wanderlog** | Opening hours on place details | Hours on places you add | Outdated Hagia Sophia and Colosseum advice (February 2026 test) |
| **Stippl** | Not stated | Weather forecast | No independent test found |
| **Layla** | Not stated | An advisor reviews web trips before booking | A museum planned on a closed day (November 2026 test) |
| **Wonderplan** | Not stated | None stated | Unsuitable picks in a family plan (April 2026 test) |
| **Google** | Google Maps, Flights, Hotels | Hours, whether a stop is open, routes | Google warns it can present wrong information as fact |
| **ChatGPT** | Web search with citations | None | Wrong cable car time (Japan); closed restaurants (Tallinn); Schengen insurance (Which?) |
| **Copilot** | Not stated | None | Flight refund rules (Which?) |
| **Perplexity** | Web search, citations on every answer | Sources you can open | Best Which? score, but its terms still warn of errors |
| **Tripadvisor** | Traveler reviews | AI review summaries | Summaries played down food-poisoning reports (Which?, July 2026) |

These aren't rare. In a survey of 1,000 US travelers for the tour company Greetwell (August 2026), 16% of those who had used AI for travel said it recommended a place, tour or activity that didn't exist, 27% got wrong prices, hours or details, and 10% were sent to a business that had closed for good. The best-known case is the "Sacred Canyon of Humantay" in Peru, reported by the BBC in 2025: two tourists paid about $160 to reach a rural road, looking for a canyon that doesn't exist.

The Natural History Museum's Christmas closure that tripped up Layla is on the list MonkeyTravel's plans are checked against. That doesn't make our planner infallible, but it's the kind of check worth asking every tool about. Whatever you use: confirm the hours of anything you'd hate to miss, especially between Christmas and New Year, look up restaurants on a map before walking there, and be wary of a plan that never shows where its information came from.

## The Verdict: Which Tool for Which Traveler

**A free day-by-day plan you can follow on the day, without an account:** MonkeyTravel. It's ours, so weigh that, but it's built for exactly this.

**Research and booking in one place:** Mindtrip.

**A person to book and stand behind the trip:** Layla, now part of Expedia.

**The most live data:** Google: Gemini and Ask Maps, plus Canvas and hotel booking in the US.

**Planning by hand with friends:** Wanderlog for real-time editing on a map, Stippl for shared costs and packing lists.

**A group that won't sign up for anything:** MonkeyTravel, where votes and the shared live day work without accounts.

**Brainstorming:** ChatGPT. **Checking facts:** Perplexity. **Other travelers' experiences:** Tripadvisor's reviews. **A plan from a form:** Wonderplan. **A draft inside Microsoft 365:** Copilot.

No single tool does it all. The setup that works in 2027 is layered: a general assistant for ideas, a planner that checks for the structure, Google Maps for hours on the day, and a booking site (or Mindtrip, or Layla) for the reservations.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best free AI trip planner in 2027?

It depends on what you need. MonkeyTravel (ours) gives you a full day-by-day plan free and without an account, checks closing days and holidays, and works in four languages. Wonderplan is free too, though it asks you to log in for its full features. Mindtrip is free and goes furthest into booking, but has no Android app. Google's Gemini is free and has the best live data. Wanderlog's and Stippl's free plans are strong for planning by hand with friends, but their AI is capped.

### Which AI trip planner is the most accurate?

None is right every time. Google's Gemini and Ask Maps have the edge on whether a place is open, because they read Google Maps. Planners that write from a model's memory need checks afterwards: MonkeyTravel re-checks closing days, big holidays and, for its listed sights, opening hours, and looks up places to eat on Google Maps once you save. Whatever you use, confirm the hours of anything you'd hate to miss.

### Can AI trip planners book flights and hotels?

Some can. Mindtrip books flights, stays, restaurants and things to do; Layla's iPhone app books flights, hotels, activities and car hire, and on its website an advisor books for you; Google's AI Mode books select US hotels; Stippl books hotels and eSIMs; Tripadvisor books hotels and experiences. MonkeyTravel, Wonderplan and Wanderlog (hotel price comparison only) don't book inside the tool, and we couldn't find booking in Copilot.

### Which AI trip planner is best for groups?

Wanderlog for real-time co-editing on a map; Stippl for shared costs, packing lists and unlimited co-planners; Mindtrip for planning in a group chat; MonkeyTravel when your group won't make accounts, since anyone with the link can vote and, during the trip, update the shared day. ChatGPT and Copilot both retired their group chats in 2026.

### Do I need an account to use an AI trip planner?

Not always. MonkeyTravel shows a full plan without one (up to five a day) and keeps it on a private link for 30 days. In a November 2026 test, Layla's website built a plan without a sign-in but its iPhone app didn't. Gemini's maps, flight and hotel features need a Google account, and most tools ask for one once you want to save or edit.

### Can AI trip planners replace travel agents?

For standard trips, AI planners handle research and the day-by-day well, for free. Agents still win on complicated multi-country trips, last-minute fixes and anything that goes wrong mid-trip, which is the gap Layla's human advisors now try to fill. More in [AI Trip Planner vs Travel Agent](/blog/ai-trip-planner-vs-travel-agent).
