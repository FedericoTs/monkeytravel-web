# Where to Go in March 2027: 12 Destinations as the World Reopens

March is the month the travel year restarts. The northern winter breaks, the deserts hit their brief perfect window before the heat, and half the closed things — mountain huts, ferry routes, seasonal hotels — start turning the lights back on. It is also the month with the widest spread between a good decision and a bad one, because two very different Marches sit inside it.

The first three weeks are shoulder season: mild, quiet, cheap. Then it turns. Easter falls on 28 March in 2027, which puts Holy Week on 21–27 March — and that week fills Andalusia, drives European prices up, and books out flights across Latin America. [Holi lands on 22 March](https://www.drikpanchang.com/hindu-festivals/holi/holi.html), inside the same week. Plan into the first three weeks and March is one of the best-value months of the year; stumble into the last one and it is one of the most expensive. Twelve places where March is the point, with the dates that matter.

## 1. Japan — Cherry Blossom, Timed Carefully

The blossom is why March is Japan's most contested month. Tokyo and Kyoto typically open in the last week of March and peak within a few days of it, but the front moves north across weeks and the exact dates shift year to year with late-winter temperatures — Kyushu goes first, Tokyo and Kyoto next, Tohoku and Hokkaido not until April or May. Our [cherry blossom season guide](/blog/japan-cherry-blossom-season-guide) tracks how the front moves; [Kyoto's official travel site](https://kyoto.travel/en) posts local timing.

**Cost reality:** $130–200/day — bloom week is the single most expensive week of the Japanese year outside New Year, and hotels price accordingly.
**The trade-off:** you cannot book peak bloom, only guess at it, and a warm February can pull it a week early. Build a trip that works regardless — early March in Kyushu, or a route with enough latitude range that you intersect the front somewhere.

## 2. Rajasthan & North India — Holi, and the Last Comfortable Weeks

[Holi falls on 22 March in 2027](https://www.drikpanchang.com/hindu-festivals/holi/holi.html) — the colour play can shift a day either side by region — and Rajasthan is the classic place to be for it. March is also the last month before north Indian heat becomes the defining feature: 28–32°C days now, and 40°C+ by May.

**Cost reality:** $35–60/day — outstanding value, though Holi week pushes Jaipur and Udaipur hotels up sharply.
**The trade-off:** Holi is boisterous and, for solo travellers and women in particular, can be overwhelming in the big tourist cities. Smaller towns and organised hotel celebrations are the calmer version, and both are legitimate ways to see it.

## 3. Andalusia — Semana Santa

Holy Week runs 21–27 March in 2027, and Seville's Semana Santa is one of Europe's great spectacles: nightly processions, brotherhoods carrying *pasos* through packed streets, the city awake until dawn. Málaga, Córdoba and Granada each run their own version.

**Cost reality:** $75–120/day in normal March weeks; Holy Week itself runs far above that with minimum-stay requirements in Seville.
**The trade-off:** this is either the reason you came or a problem to avoid — there is no middle. During Holy Week the centre of Seville is impassable at procession times and rooms book many months out. The first three weeks of March give you the same city, mild and genuinely quiet.

## 4. Patagonia — The Calm End of Summer

March is Patagonia's best-kept secret. The famous wind drops noticeably from its January peak, the *lenga* forests turn red and gold, and the summer crowds have gone home — while the refugios and park services are still running. Days shorten, but the walking conditions are often better than in peak season.

**Cost reality:** $100–160/day — below January's peak, and hut availability is dramatically easier.
**The trade-off:** the season is ending, so services wind down through the month and some routes and refugios close from late March. Check current closing dates for anything you are building a trip around, and expect genuinely cold nights.

## 5. Jordan — Spring on the Desert Trails

March is when Jordan is at its best: 20–24°C in the Petra basin, wildflowers across the Dana reserve, and hiking weather that neither the summer heat nor the winter cold allows. The Jordan Trail's most popular sections run in exactly this window.

**Cost reality:** $80–130/day — the Jordan Pass bundles the visa and site entry and pays for itself in about two days.
**The trade-off:** desert nights are still cold, and March can bring sudden rain that causes flash flooding in the siqs and canyons. Check conditions before any wadi hike; the danger is real and locally well signposted.

## 6. Morocco — Green Atlas, Empty Coast

March catches Morocco between winter and the furnace: Marrakech in the low-to-mid 20s°C, the High Atlas still snow-capped but the passes opening, and the valleys green from winter rain. The Atlantic coast — Essaouira, Taghazout — is windy and bright, and the desert south is comfortable rather than punishing.

**Cost reality:** $50–85/day; a riad with a courtyard remains the classic mid-range stay.
**The trade-off:** it is shoulder season, so the weather is changeable — warm afternoons, cold nights, and occasional heavy rain that turns unpaved routes into mud. Ramadan may also fall in this period in some years; check the current dates, as opening hours and restaurant service change significantly.

## 7. Crete & the Peloponnese — Wildflowers and Empty Ruins

Greece in March is a walking and archaeology destination rather than a beach one, and it is spectacular at it: 17–20°C, hillsides covered in wildflowers, and Knossos, Mycenae or Olympia with almost nobody in them. Orthodox Easter falls much later in 2027 — 2 May — so March stays quiet right through.

**Cost reality:** $60–100/day — low-season pricing on an expensive country.
**The trade-off:** the sea is cold, many island ferries run winter schedules, and a good share of island hotels and tavernas do not open until April or May. Stay on Crete or the mainland, where things are open year-round.

## 8. Utah & Arizona — The Desert's Perfect Window

Zion, Bryce, Arches, Canyonlands and the Grand Canyon's South Rim in March: daytime in the high teens to low 20s°C, cold clear nights, and none of the summer heat that makes midday hiking genuinely dangerous. Spring runoff also means the desert washes and waterfalls actually have water.

**Cost reality:** $110–170/day — a rental car is mandatory and is the biggest single line.
**The trade-off:** it overlaps American spring break, so mid-to-late March fills Zion and the Grand Canyon and the shuttle queues get long. Bryce sits above 2,400m and still gets snow. Go early in the month or pick the quieter parks.

## 9. Iceland — The Last Good Aurora Month

March is the sweet spot at the end of the aurora season: still dark enough for strong displays, but with around twelve hours of daylight by the equinox — enough to actually drive the south coast, walk a glacier and see the waterfalls in the same trip. [Visit Iceland](https://www.visiticeland.com/) is the official planning source.

**Cost reality:** $150–230/day — still expensive, still cheaper than summer.
**The trade-off:** March is a stormy month and road closures are common, particularly in the north and the highlands, which remain shut. Ice caves also become unreliable as melt begins. Build slack, and do not plan a tight ring-road loop.

## 10. Vietnam's Centre — The Dry Season Arrives

Hoi An, Da Nang and Hue spend the winter grey and drizzly, and March is when that breaks: rising temperatures, falling rain, and the beaches becoming usable again. It is the one month when the whole country is reasonably good at the same time, which is rare.

**Cost reality:** $35–60/day — Vietnam's value is unchanged, and central Vietnam is cheaper than either end.
**The trade-off:** early March can still catch the tail of the drizzle in Hue, and the north is only just warming. If your trip is centre-and-south, March is ideal; if it is Hanoi-and-Sapa, April is better.

## 11. Victoria Falls — Peak Flow

The Zambezi's rains fall from November to March, and the water arrives at the falls with a lag — so March into April is when Victoria Falls runs at its most enormous. This is the version that generates its own weather and soaks you from the viewing paths.

**Cost reality:** $90–150/day across Livingstone (Zambia) and Victoria Falls town (Zimbabwe).
**The trade-off:** peak flow means peak spray, and the Zambian side in particular can be so drenched that you see mist rather than water. Devil's Pool is closed at high water. If you want to see the rock face and swim at the edge, that is a September–December trip, not this one.

## 12. Nepal — Spring Trekking Opens

The spring season starts in March: stable weather, warming days, and the rhododendron forests along the Annapurna and Langtang routes coming into bloom from mid-month. Views are hazier than the crystalline post-monsoon autumn, but the trails are open and the passes are clearing.

**Cost reality:** $40–70/day on the trail including teahouses, permits and a guide.
**The trade-off:** March haze is a real limitation for photography — autumn is sharper. Higher passes may still hold snow early in the month, and Everest Base Camp is busier in spring than in autumn because of the climbing season.

## Practical Tips for March Travel

- **The month splits in two: before and after 21 March.** Holy Week (21–27 March) and Easter (28 March) drive European and Latin American prices up hard, and Holi falls on the 22nd. The first three weeks are shoulder season; the last one is not.
- **Cherry blossom cannot be booked, only bet on.** Tokyo and Kyoto usually peak in the last days of March, but the date moves with the winter. Build a route with enough north–south range that you meet the front somewhere rather than staking a whole trip on one city and one week.
- **March is the desert's month.** Jordan, Morocco, Rajasthan, Utah and Arizona are all in the narrow window between winter cold and summer heat. By May, several of them are genuinely unpleasant.
- **Check what has actually reopened.** March is a transition month, and seasonal hotels, ferries, mountain huts and park roads reopen on wildly different dates. Confirm rather than assume, especially in Greece, Iceland and Patagonia.
- **Compare it with the months either side before you lock in.** [February](/blog/where-to-go-in-february) is cheaper up north and [April](/blog/where-to-go-in-april) is warmer — see how the [2026 travel calendar](/blog/2026-travel-calendar) stacks up before you commit to dates.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Where is the best place to go in March 2027?

For a single unmissable event, Japan's cherry blossom in the last week of March and Seville's Semana Santa (21–27 March). For the best weather-to-price ratio, Jordan, Morocco and Rajasthan are all in their perfect window. For quiet, Crete and the Peloponnese are beautiful and nearly empty. And Patagonia in March is calmer and cheaper than in January.

### When is Easter 2027 and does it affect March travel?

Easter Sunday is 28 March 2027, so Holy Week runs 21–27 March. That week raises prices and fills accommodation across Spain, Italy and Latin America, and it is when Seville's Semana Santa takes over the city. Orthodox Easter is separate and much later — 2 May 2027 — so Greece stays quiet through March.

### Where is it warm in March?

Reliably hot: Vietnam's centre and south, Rajasthan, and Victoria Falls. Comfortably warm rather than hot: Jordan, Morocco and Andalusia in the high teens to low 20s°C — ideal for walking, not for swimming. The Southern Hemisphere is cooling into autumn, so Patagonia is mild rather than warm.

### Which destinations should I avoid in March?

Northern Laos and Thailand are in the agricultural burning season and the haze can be severe. Most Greek islands and Mediterranean beach resorts are still closed. Iceland's highlands and north are unreliable for road travel. And anywhere in Spain during Holy Week if the processions are not what you came for.
