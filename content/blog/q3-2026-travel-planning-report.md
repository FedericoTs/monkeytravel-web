# The Q3 2026 Travel Planning Report: What 1,651 Real Trip Plans Reveal

Most travel statistics measure what people *search* or what they *book*. Almost none measure the step in between — the moment someone sits down with real dates and actually plans a trip. That's the step we can see, and this quarter it tells a clearer story than either of the other two.

This is the summer edition of our planning-data series — in July we published a [deep dive on the most-planned destinations of 2026](/blog/most-planned-destinations-2026); this report widens the lens to *how* people plan, not just *where*. It covers **1,651 trip-planning sessions started on MonkeyTravel between May 31 and August 4, 2026**.

Three findings stand out: Tokyo keeps the crown, the one-week trip has become the default unit of travel, and planning alone is now the minority behavior.

## How We Read the Data

Everything below comes from **aggregated, anonymized planning data** — the destinations, trip lengths, and group setups people chose while building itineraries. We report proportions and rankings, never individual trips, and no traveler is identifiable in any figure. Sessions are counted once each. Where travelers skipped a question, we say so and compute percentages only over those who answered. This isn't a survey of intentions; it's a record of plans people actually built.

1,651 sessions is a meaningful sample of real planning behavior, not a global census. We'd rather tell you exactly what our data says than borrow bigger numbers that measure something vaguer.

## Finding 1: Tokyo Holds #1 — and It Still Isn't Close

The summer ranking, by share of planning sessions:

1. [Tokyo](/destinations/tokyo)
2. [Paris](/destinations/paris)
3. [New York](/destinations/new-york)
4. [Bangkok](/destinations/bangkok)
5. [Bali](/destinations/bali)
6. [London](/destinations/london)
7. [Rome](/destinations/rome)
8. [Barcelona](/destinations/barcelona)
9. [Kyoto](/destinations/kyoto)
10. [Santorini](/destinations/santorini)

**Tokyo drew 36% more planning sessions than Paris** this summer — the same #1 it held in [our July ranking](/blog/most-planned-destinations-2026), with the gap intact. Add [Kyoto](/destinations/kyoto) and Japan accounts for more planning activity than any other country's cities in the top ten.

The structural pattern also held: **three of the top five are in Asia**. European capitals still fill out the list, but the center of gravity for 2026 planning has clearly shifted east — consistent with what we found in [1.1 million travel searches](/blog/travel-planning-trends-2026) earlier this year. For per-city breakdowns and the surprises further down the table, the [July deep dive](/blog/most-planned-destinations-2026) is the fuller read.

## Finding 2: The One-Week Trip Is the Unit of Travel

Across every session that set dates:

- **Median trip length: 6 days**
- **Mean: 7.1 days** (a long tail of two-week-plus trips pulls the average up)
- **46% of all trips fall in the 4-to-7-day band**

Nearly half of everything planned fits the same shape: fly out on a Thursday or Friday, come back the following week. It's the paid-vacation trip, the "one week of annual leave" trip — and it lines up almost exactly with the 4-to-7-night pattern we found in search data last quarter, which is the kind of agreement between two independent datasets that makes us trust both more.

If you're planning one of these, the constraint isn't the destination — it's making six days count. That's what day-by-day planning is for: see the [5-day Italy itinerary](/blog/5-day-italy-itinerary) or the [7-day Bali plan](/blog/bali-7-day-itinerary) for the shape of it.

## Finding 3: Travel Is a Group Sport Now

When travelers told us who they were traveling with, the split was decisive:

- **69% were planning a trip with other people**
- **31% were planning solo**

Seven in ten trips have a crew behind them — a partner, a friend group, a family. Yet almost every travel tool is still built for one person with one screen making decisions alone, which is exactly why group trips die in the group chat: forty messages about dates, someone who won't commit, and a plan that never quite exists anywhere.

That mismatch — group trips, solo tools — is the single biggest gap in how travel planning works in 2026. It's also why we built [voting and shared itineraries](/group-trip-planner) into MonkeyTravel rather than bolting them on. If your last group trip took three weeks of chat messages to produce zero bookings, our [guide to planning a group trip](/blog/how-to-plan-a-group-trip) is the antidote.

## What This Means If You're Planning a Trip Right Now

**Book Japan early.** When one city tops the planning charts two quarters running, flights and hotels feel it. Tokyo in shoulder season is still the smart move, but "shoulder season" gets more crowded every quarter.

**Design for six days.** The most common trip is short enough that a wasted morning matters. Anchor the two or three things you'd regret missing, and leave the rest loose.

**If you're the group's planner, stop being the group's bottleneck.** Share the plan, let people vote, and let the itinerary be the single source of truth instead of the chat scroll.
