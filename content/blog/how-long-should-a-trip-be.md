# How Long Should a Trip Be? Real Data Says 6 Days

"How many days do I need in ___?" is one of the most-asked questions in travel — and almost every answer you'll find is a guess. We have something better: **1,651 real trip plans built on MonkeyTravel between May 31 and August 4, 2026**, each with actual dates attached.

Here's what real trips look like, and how to decide whether yours should match.

## What the Data Says

- **The median trip is 6 days.** Half of all trips planned this summer were shorter, half longer.
- **46% of all trips fall in the 4-to-7-day band.** Nearly one in two trips is the classic "one week of leave" shape.
- **The average is 7.1 days** — higher than the median because a long tail of two-week-plus itineraries pulls it up. Most people plan a week; a committed minority plans a lot more.

The pattern is remarkably stable: we saw the same 4-to-7-day dominance in [search data covering 1.1 million queries](/blog/travel-planning-trends-2026), and it holds again in actual plans. This is simply how modern travel is shaped — by the workweek, not by the destination.

## The Question That Actually Decides Trip Length

Before "how many days does Rome need?", ask: **is this trip a sprint, a sample, or a stay?**

**A sprint (3–4 days)** is one city, no day trips, and a shortlist you'd defend in court. It works brilliantly for compact, dense cities — [London](/destinations/london), [Barcelona](/destinations/barcelona), [Paris](/destinations/paris) if it's not your first time. It fails in places where the airport transfer alone eats half a day.

**A sample (5–7 days)** is the data's sweet spot and the shape most trips actually take. One anchor city plus one contrast — [Rome](/destinations/rome) plus a countryside day, [Tokyo](/destinations/tokyo) plus a day in [Kyoto](/destinations/kyoto), [Bangkok](/destinations/bangkok) plus a temple town. Our [5-day Italy itinerary](/blog/5-day-italy-itinerary) is built on exactly this logic.

**A stay (8+ days)** is where per-day cost drops and the trip changes character — you stop collecting sights and start having routines. [Bali](/destinations/bali) trips in our data skew this way, and it's the right shape for anywhere you're going to *be* rather than *see*. The [7-day Bali itinerary](/blog/bali-7-day-itinerary) sits at the threshold.

## Three Rules From the Data

**1. Subtract your travel days before counting.** A "7-day trip" with two long-haul flights is a 5-day trip. The most common planning mistake we see is itineraries that schedule the arrival day like a full day.

**2. Long-haul needs a minimum.** If the flight is over 8 hours, fewer than 6 days on the ground means jet lag eats a third of your trip. This is why Tokyo trips in our data run longer than European city breaks — planners intuit it.

**3. Groups need buffer days.** Solo travelers can sprint; groups can't. If you're one of the **69% planning with other people**, add a day for the simple reason that five people move slower than one. (More on that in our [group travel statistics](/blog/group-travel-statistics-2026).)

## So: How Long Should *Your* Trip Be?

The honest answer from 1,651 real plans: **six days is the shape most trips want to be** — long enough for one place done properly plus one surprise, short enough to fit real life.

If you can't decide, plan the 6-day version and see if it feels crowded or empty. [Build one free](/trips/new) — it takes about a minute, and it's much easier to judge a real day-by-day plan than an imaginary one.
