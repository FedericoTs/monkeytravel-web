# Where to Go in January 2027: 12 Destinations for the Year's Quietest Month

The first week of January is the hangover. Everything after it is the bargain. Once the holiday traffic clears — and it clears hard, usually by the 6th or 7th — the Northern Hemisphere enters its deadest travel weeks of the year, and prices fall through the floor. Europe's cities are cold and half-empty, long-haul fares drop, and hotels that were surcharging on the 28th of December are quietly discounting three weeks later. Nothing else on the calendar buys this much for this little.

The other half of the story is that January is not winter everywhere. Below the equator it is the top of summer: Patagonia is warm and light until eleven at night, New Zealand's rivers are swimmable, Cape Town is in full season. Across the tropics the northeast monsoon has settled into a long dry spell, so Thailand's Andaman coast, southern Vietnam, and Sri Lanka's southwest are as reliable as they get all year. And in the far north, the polar night is ending — Lapland gets its blue light back while the aurora is still at full strength. Twelve places where January is the point, not the penalty.

## 1. Thailand's Andaman Coast — The Driest Month

If you only get one week of guaranteed sun a year, January on the Andaman side is where to spend it. The northeast monsoon has pushed the rain off Phuket, Krabi, and the Phi Phi and Similan islands, the sea flattens and clears, and the humidity that makes Bangkok hard work in April is simply absent. Daytime settles around 32°C with cool nights inland — the most comfortable the country gets.

**Cost reality:** $45–80/day mid-range — this is peak season and it is priced like it, especially the first half of the month.
**The trade-off:** everyone else knows this too. Ferries, dive boats and the best beachfront rooms book out, and the Similans in particular have visitor caps. Reserve boats and island transfers before you fly, not on arrival.

## 2. Patagonia — The Top of Summer

January is Patagonia's warmest month and its longest, with usable light past 22:00. Torres del Paine, El Chaltén, and the Carretera Austral are fully open, refugios are staffed, and the wildflowers on the steppe are out. This is the month the W and O circuits are genuinely pleasant rather than an endurance test.

**Cost reality:** $110–170/day — Patagonia is expensive in any month, and January is the top of it. Refugio beds and park buses are the budget-breakers.
**The trade-off:** the wind. January is also peak wind season, with gusts that can stop you walking on exposed ridgelines, and Torres del Paine's refugios sell out months ahead. Book the huts the moment the season opens, and build a spare day for weather.

## 3. Hokkaido, Japan — The Most Reliable Powder on Earth

Niseko, Furano, and Rusutsu get hit by Siberian air crossing the Sea of Japan, and the result is snow that falls in absurd quantity and stays dry. January is the deepest month. Beyond the lifts there are onsen in the snow, the Sapporo food scene, and drift ice starting to arrive on the Okhotsk coast late in the month.

**Cost reality:** $120–180/day — lift passes and Niseko accommodation are the expense; Furano and the smaller resorts are meaningfully cheaper for similar snow.
**The trade-off:** it snows almost every day, which is the appeal and also the problem — views are rare and road transfers slow. If you want blue-sky skiing, go elsewhere; if you want the snow itself, nowhere is better.

## 4. Finnish Lapland — Aurora, With the Light Coming Back

December's polar night is over by January, and the return of a few hours of blue daylight makes Lapland far more usable than the month before while the aurora is still at full strength. Add husky and reindeer trips, ice-fishing and snowshoe country outside Rovaniemi and Inari, and the cold-season infrastructure ([Visit Finland](https://www.visitfinland.com/en/) is the official starting point) is genuinely built for visitors.

**Cost reality:** $150–220/day — glass igloos and guided aurora trips are what push it; a cabin plus a rental car is far cheaper and often better for chasing clear sky.
**The trade-off:** it is properly cold, routinely −20°C and colder, and the aurora is weather, not a booking. Give yourself three or four nights minimum; a two-night trip is a coin flip.

## 5. Sri Lanka — Dry Season on the Right Coast

Sri Lanka runs two monsoons on opposite schedules, and January is the southwest's turn to be dry. Galle, Mirissa, Weligama and Tangalle get sun and swimmable sea, the hill country around Ella and Nuwara Eliya is clear for walking and tea estates, and blue whale season off Mirissa is at its peak.

**Cost reality:** $35–65/day — one of the strongest value-to-quality ratios anywhere on this list, even in high season.
**The trade-off:** the east coast (Arugam Bay, Trincomalee) is on the opposite calendar and is wet and flat now. Pick a coast and stay on it rather than trying to do both in one trip.

## 6. New Zealand — Everything Open at Once

January is high summer: the Great Walks are fully open and staffed, the Abel Tasman and Marlborough Sounds are swimmable, and the South Island's long evenings give you fourteen usable hours. Everything that is closed or weather-dependent in shoulder season is running now.

**Cost reality:** $120–180/day — this is also New Zealand's domestic holiday month, so campervans, ferries and Queenstown beds are at their most expensive.
**The trade-off:** you are sharing it with the entire country. New Zealanders take January off, so the popular campsites and Great Walk huts book out further ahead than at any other time. Reserve huts the day the ballot opens or shift to February.

## 7. Costa Rica's Pacific — Dry Season, Concentrated Wildlife

January is the middle of the Pacific dry season: Guanacaste, Manuel Antonio and the Nicoya beaches get consistent sun, trails are firm, and — the part people miss — the wildlife concentrates around the remaining water, which makes it far easier to see. Green season is prettier; dry season is better for actually spotting things.

**Cost reality:** $80–130/day — Costa Rica is not the bargain it once was, and January is its high season.
**The trade-off:** the Caribbean side (Puerto Viejo, Tortuguero) follows a different, wetter pattern, and the Pacific northwest can be genuinely dusty and brown by late in the season. Go for wildlife and beaches, not for lush jungle photos.

## 8. Oman — The Cool Season at Its Best

Muscat sits in the mid-20s°C, the wadis are swimmable without being cold, and the Hajar mountains and Wahiba Sands are comfortable for the first time since autumn. Oman's version of a beach holiday comes with forts, canyons and empty roads, and January is the most forgiving month to drive it.

**Cost reality:** $90–140/day — a 4x4 is close to mandatory for the interior and is the line item that moves the total.
**The trade-off:** distances are long and public transport is thin. Nights in the desert and at altitude get cold, and Salalah in the south is on a different calendar entirely — its green season is the summer monsoon, not now.

## 9. The Canary Islands — Europe's Only Reliable Winter Sun

Nowhere else in Europe delivers 20–22°C and dependable sun in January. Tenerife and Gran Canaria carry the flights and the resorts; La Palma, La Gomera and El Hierro carry the walking, the volcanic landscapes and the quiet. Four hours from most of northern Europe, and a fraction of the cost of going long-haul for warmth.

**Cost reality:** $70–110/day — cheap by European standards, and January flight prices are the lowest of the winter.
**The trade-off:** it is warm, not hot, and the Atlantic is bracing. The big southern resorts are busy with long-stay northern European visitors all winter; go west and north on any island and that thins out immediately.

## 10. Rajasthan & North India — The Most Walkable Month

January is when Jaipur, Jodhpur, Udaipur and Jaisalmer are actually comfortable: 22–25°C days, cool nights, clear light, and none of the heat that makes fort-climbing punishing from March onward. The desert is at its best, and the Ranthambore and Bharatpur wildlife parks are in their prime window.

**Cost reality:** $35–60/day — among the best value on this list, though heritage-hotel rooms rise sharply in peak season.
**The trade-off:** north Indian winter fog is real and routinely delays flights and trains around Delhi, sometimes by many hours. Build slack into any tight connection, and expect genuinely cold nights in the desert.

## 11. Vietnam's South & the Mekong — Dry, Warm, Cheap

Ho Chi Minh City, the Mekong Delta and Phu Quoc are in the dry half of their year: warm, low rain, and reliable. Con Dao is at its calmest for diving, and the Delta's floating markets are easy to reach on firm roads.

**Cost reality:** $35–60/day — Vietnam remains one of the cheapest good-food countries on earth, and January does not change that much.
**The trade-off:** the north is the opposite story. Hanoi, Sapa and Ha Long can be grey, drizzly and genuinely cold in January — a very different trip from the one the photos promise. Do not try to do the whole country this month.

## 12. Northern Europe's Cities — The Cheapest They Ever Are

Copenhagen, Vienna, Berlin, Amsterdam and Edinburgh in mid-January are as cheap as they get: post-holiday flight prices, hotels discounting hard, and museums and restaurants that need bookings in June taking walk-ins. Cold and dark, yes — but these are indoor cities, and the indoors is empty.

**Cost reality:** $90–150/day — the same cities run 40–60% higher in summer for a worse experience of their museums.
**The trade-off:** short daylight (often under eight hours) and weather that will not cooperate. Plan around interiors — galleries, thermal baths, concert halls, long lunches — and treat any clear afternoon as a bonus.

## Practical Tips for January Travel

- **Skip the first week.** The holiday premium persists through roughly 6 January in most of Europe, and the drop after it is steep. A departure on the 10th rather than the 2nd can halve the fare on the same route.
- **Pick your hemisphere and commit.** January splits the planet more sharply than any other month. Chasing both — a European city break plus a tropical beach — usually means paying long-haul peak-season prices for the second half.
- **Watch the two-coast countries.** Sri Lanka, Vietnam, Costa Rica, Thailand and Oman all have regions on opposite weather calendars in January. Our [monsoon-season guide](/blog/monsoon-season-where-to-go-and-avoid) maps which side is dry.
- **Book the Southern Hemisphere earlier than feels necessary.** January is domestic holiday season in Argentina, Chile, New Zealand and South Africa, so huts, ferries, campervans and park permits sell out to locals long before international visitors start looking.
- **Compare it against the shoulder months before locking in.** If your dates can move, [February](/blog/where-to-go-in-february) keeps most of January's weather with fewer domestic crowds in the south — see how the [2026 travel calendar](/blog/2026-travel-calendar) lines up first.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Where is the best place to go in January 2027?

It depends which hemisphere you want. For guaranteed heat and sun, Thailand's Andaman coast and Sri Lanka's southwest are the most reliable. For summer, Patagonia and New Zealand are at their absolute peak. For snow, Hokkaido has the deepest, driest powder anywhere. And for value, Northern Europe's cities are cheaper in mid-January than at any other point in the year.

### Where is it warm in January?

Reliably above 28°C: Thailand's Andaman coast, southern Vietnam, Sri Lanka's south and west, and the Pacific side of Costa Rica. Milder but dependably sunny: Oman and the Canary Islands in the low-to-mid 20s°C. In the Southern Hemisphere, Patagonia, New Zealand and Cape Town are in full summer.

### Is January a cheap month to travel?

After the first week, yes — one of the cheapest of the year in the Northern Hemisphere. Flights and hotels in Europe drop sharply once the holiday traffic clears around 6 January. The exception is anywhere in the Southern Hemisphere, where January is peak domestic holiday season and prices run the other way.

### Which destinations should I avoid in January?

Northern Vietnam, which is cold and grey; Sri Lanka's east coast and Thailand's Gulf islands, both on the wrong monsoon; the Caribbean's cheapest weeks are gone with holiday pricing still lingering; and most of the Mediterranean, which is closed, cold and wet without being cheap enough to compensate.
