# Layla vs Mindtrip 2026: Which AI Trip Planner Is Worth It?

These two get compared constantly, and the comparison usually misses the thing that actually decides it.

It is not features. Both will take "five days in Lisbon, mid-range budget" and hand you something structured. The difference is **who pays for the plan** — and that shapes everything downstream: what you see for free, what the tool nudges you toward, and where each one quietly stops being useful.

## The short version

**Layla** sells the plan — or has. Early-2026 reviews found the free tier gives you a trip overview, with the actual day-by-day itinerary and PDF export behind a paid tier at roughly $50 a year; Layla's own June-2026 copy says generation is free and Premium buys live pricing. The line has moved, so check. Since 31 July 2026 Layla has also belonged to Expedia Group.

**Mindtrip** gives the plan away. No meaningful paywall on the core itinerary — bookings are handled by third-party partners, and how Mindtrip earns from them isn't something it documents.

Neither is a trick. They are two honest answers to "how does a free AI travel tool survive," and they suit different people.

## Pricing, precisely

| | Free tier | Paid |
|---|---|---|
| **Layla** | Trip overview, map and a total price in early-2026 reviews; Layla's June-2026 copy says full itinerary generation — check | **Premium, about $49/yr** ($49.99/yr or $9.99/mo on iOS) — live pricing and PriceLock alerts, PDF export, unlimited trips, and the day-by-day detail wherever the free tier stops short |
| **Mindtrip** | Full core itinerary | No consumer subscription; bookings go through partner sites |

Two things worth noticing.

First, don't assume Layla's free tier gives you the trip. At times in 2026 it has been a **preview** — the shape of a trip, not the trip — with the day-by-day behind Premium; Layla's own June-2026 copy says generation is free. If the day-by-day plan is the thing you came for, check what the free tier shows you today before you treat Layla as free.

Second, "free" for Mindtrip means the product is built around booking. That is not sinister — but a planner whose flow ends in a partner reservation will always be a little more enthusiastic about the booking step than a planner whose flow does not.

## Where Layla wins

**Live pricing inside the plan.** Layla connects to Skyscanner, Booking.com and GetYourGuide, so the numbers next to a flight or hotel are real prices rather than a model's guess at a price. If you have been burned by an AI confidently inventing a €90 hotel rate, this is the fix.

**It commits to an itinerary.** Layla produces a logical, bookable sequence rather than a pile of suggestions. For a traveller who wants one plan to react to rather than a menu, that is the right shape.

**Clear upgrade path.** Premium is about $49 a year on Layla's site ($49.99 a year or $9.99 a month on iOS) — published prices, even if what the free tier includes has moved.

## Where Mindtrip wins

**The full plan costs nothing.** The core itinerary is not gated. For a lot of people that is the entire decision.

**Booking without leaving.** In May 2026 Mindtrip added in-chat flight booking through a Sabre and PayPal partnership, and in July 2026 conversational hotel search and booking (Mindtrip Stays), on top of restaurant and experience booking via partners including Priceline and Viator. Itinerary to reservation without a new tab is genuinely useful.

**Depth of place data.** Mindtrip is built on a large curated points-of-interest database, which makes its suggestions read less like generic model output and more like something a person assembled.

## The catch nobody mentions

**Mindtrip asks you to log in before you see anything.** As of mid-2026 the planner sits behind a Google or Apple sign-in, so you create an account before you know whether the output is any good. Layla will show you an overview first — but the part you actually want is the part you pay for.

So both tools ask for something up front. One asks for your account, the other asks for your card. That is the honest framing, and it is worth knowing before you invest an evening in either.

And since 31 July 2026 Layla belongs to Expedia Group. A planner owned by a booking company has an interest in where your bookings land, so read its hotel and flight suggestions with that in mind.

## What neither does especially well

**Group planning.** Both are built around one person's conversation. If the real problem is four friends who cannot agree on dates, neither tool is aimed at you.

**Languages.** No longer a shared gap: as of September 2026 Mindtrip's app lists eight languages, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese included. Layla is English-first, so check before you commit.

**A day you can actually walk.** This is the recurring gap across the whole category. A good day is three or four stops that sit near each other, in a sensible order, with realistic gaps for eating and moving. A list of excellent places scattered across a city is not that, and most tools hand you the list.

## So which one

**Choose Layla** if live, bookable prices are the point and roughly $50 a year is a fair trade for a finished itinerary. Budget for Premium — at times in 2026 the free tier has been only a preview.

**Choose Mindtrip** if you want the full plan for nothing, you book inside the app anyway, and creating an account first does not bother you.

**Choose neither** if what you want is a complete day-by-day schedule you can read before signing up for anything. That is the gap both leave open.

Whichever you pick, run the same test on the output: take day one and trace the stops on a map. If the route zigzags across the city, the tool gave you a list and called it a plan.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Layla free?**
There is a free tier, but where it stops has moved during 2026. Early-2026 reviews found it gives you an overview, a map and a total price — not the day-by-day plan, which sat with PDF export, unlimited trips and live pricing behind Premium at about $49 a year. Layla's own June-2026 copy says free now covers full itinerary generation and Premium buys live pricing and PriceLock. Check what you get. If Layla offers you a trial (early-2026 reviews described a three-day one), Premium auto-renews after it.

**Is Mindtrip really free?**
Yes for the core itinerary — there is no consumer subscription. Bookings go through third-party partners (in-chat flight booking arrived in May 2026 via Sabre and PayPal, hotel booking in July 2026), and how Mindtrip earns from them isn't documented. You do need to create an account to use it.

**Which is better for booking?**
Mindtrip, for booking inside the app. Layla is stronger on live price accuracy inside the plan because it pulls from Skyscanner, Booking.com and GetYourGuide.

**Which is better for group trips?**
Neither, really. Both are built around a single planner's conversation. If group coordination is the actual problem, look at tools built for it — ours included.

**Do either work in languages other than English?**
Mindtrip does: as of September 2026 its app lists eight languages, including Spanish, Italian and Portuguese. Layla is English-first — check its current language support before committing.

**What if I do not want to pay or create an account?**
Then neither fits cleanly: Layla gates the plan behind a tier and Mindtrip gates it behind a login. Tools that show a full itinerary with neither are a short list, ours included.
