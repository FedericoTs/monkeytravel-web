# Solar Eclipse 2027: Where to See Totality and How to Plan the Trip

On Monday 2 August 2027 the Moon's full shadow crosses southern Spain, northern Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Yemen and Somalia. For the easy trip, go to the Strait of Gibraltar: up to about 4 min 50 s of darkness in mid-morning, in beach weather. For the long one, go to Luxor: 6 min 20 s at 13:02 local time, at 41°C (106°F).

This guide is for anyone deciding where to stand, and for readers at home who want to know what they'll see. Check your exact spot before you pay: Seville, Granada, Almería city, Tunis, Lampedusa, Aswan and Hurghada all miss totality, some by only a few kilometres.

Every date, time and rule here was read on the official page on 6 November 2026: Spain's National Geographic Institute (IGN) and eclipse commission, NASA and national travel advisers. Press figures are marked "reported by". Times are local with the zone named; CEST is Spanish summer time, UTC+2.

## Where the path of totality goes, country by country

[NASA's path table](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/SEpath/SEpath2001/SE2027Aug02Tpath.html) shows a track 236 km (147 miles) wide at the Strait and 258 km (160 miles) wide in Egypt.

- **Spain:** Ceuta, Melilla, almost all of Cádiz province, much of Málaga province and the southern edges of Granada and Almería, according to IGN. Totality falls between about 10:45 and 10:53 CEST.
- **Gibraltar:** 4:26 from 10:45 CEST.
- **Morocco:** Tangier 4:50, Tetouan 4:51, Chefchaouen 4:13, Al Hoceima 4:25, Nador 4:14.
- **Algeria:** Oran 5:07, Tlemcen 3:09. Algiers stops at 99.8%.
- **Tunisia:** Sfax 5:39, Djerba 4:40, El Jem 4:24, Mahdia 2:48.
- **Libya:** Benghazi 6:09. Tripoli sits on the southern limit and just misses on NASA's explorer.
- **Egypt:** Siwa 5:28, Bahariya 6:17, Sohag 6:22, Luxor 6:20, Marsa Alam 5:31. Greatest eclipse is at 10:06 UT in the desert east of Luxor, and the longest totality anywhere, 6 min 23 s, lies on the centre line between Asyut and Luxor.
- **Saudi Arabia:** Jeddah 5:56, Mecca 5:08.
- **Yemen and Somalia:** Sana'a 2:16, Bosaso 3:58, then out over the Indian Ocean.

[EclipseWise's](https://eclipsewise.com/solar/SEprime/2001-2100/SE2027Aug02Tprime.html) country list omits Tunisia, but NASA's table runs the centre line across it. Portugal, Italy and France get no totality, and the path misses Porto Santo and Madeira by about 70 km (Eclipsophile).

## Near the edge: who gets totality and who misses

This is where people lose money. Totality shrinks fast near the limit and then vanishes, and 99% is not totality: outside the path, the [American Astronomical Society](https://eclipse.aas.org/eye-safety) says, it's never safe to look without a filter.

A town's figure covers one point, and a municipality can straddle the line: Almería's centre misses but its Cabo de Gata end doesn't. That may be why [Euro Weekly News](https://euroweeklynews.com/2026/08/21/almerias-2027-total-eclipse-already-pushing-hotel-prices-and-demand-through-the-roof/) lists Almería city, Níjar and Berja as in totality, when IGN's table doesn't.

Figures move fast near the edge, as Jerez shows, and NASA says the Moon's uneven limb can move the limits by 1 to 3 km. If you're within a few kilometres of the line, move, and check your street on [IGN's eclipse viewer](https://visualizadores.ign.es/eclipses/2027) before you pay.

Demand doesn't follow the map, either. Reported by [QuiFinanza](https://quifinanza.it/lifestyle/travel-economy/eclissi-2027-lampedusa-altre-citta/1016621/), Lampedusa is sold out, though NASA's limits put it about 16 km (10 miles) outside the path. Reported by [Logos Press](https://logos-pres.md/en/news/tourists-are-already-planning-trips-to-see-the-solar-eclipse-in-2027/), relaying Euronews, 87% of Seville listings on Booking.com were gone by August 2026. [Egypt Independent](https://www.egyptindependent.com/?p=2827383) mentions viewing events at Abu Simbel.

## Cádiz province: Conil, Vejer, Barbate and Tarifa

**Why here:** IGN's table gives Tarifa 4:38, Barbate 4:16, Vejer 4:05 and Conil 4:01, all from about 10:45 CEST, with the Sun 38° up in the east. It's over at about noon, so you keep the afternoon for the beach.

**When to go:** Arrive by Sunday 1 August at the latest. Spain's ["1 August" traffic operation](https://www.lamoncloa.gob.es/serviciosdeprensa/notasprensa/interior/Paginas/2026/300626-trafico-operacion-verano-2026.aspx) ran from 31 July to 2 August in 2026; the 2027 dates aren't announced.

**What to book:** Sleep a little inland if you can. Eclipsophile's survey gave Cádiz city 88% of sun-visible days but Barbate, Atlanterra and Tarifa only 74% in its text (its chart reads about 77%), because of morning sea cloud. It advises going inland, where cloud averages under 15%. Benalup-Casas Viejas (3:50), Los Barrios (4:19) and Castellar (4:04) all keep a long totality.

**Getting there and cost:** Jerez airport is just inside the path, and the [C-1 train](https://www.aena.es/en/jerez/getting-there/trains.html) reaches Cádiz in about 57 minutes. You'll want a car for Conil, Vejer and Tarifa.

Asking prices are extreme. Reported by [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/travel/2026/08/27/2027-eclipse-sends-prices-soaring-up-to-33129-a-night-in-el-palmar), a six-person villa in El Palmar asked €33,129 for the night and three Tarifa properties €11,090 to €13,260. Reported by [Andalucía Informa](https://andaluciainforma.es/?p=34914), the cheapest two nights in Cádiz city cost €3,600, against €450 a week later. Those are listings, not what people pay.

**The trade-off:** Highs are mild, at [24.5°C in Tarifa](https://www.aemet.es/es/serviciosclimaticos/datosclimatologicos/valoresclimatologicos?l=6001&k=and) and [27.9°C in Cádiz](https://www.aemet.es/es/serviciosclimaticos/datosclimatologicos/valoresclimatologicos?l=5973&k=and), but asking prices are the steepest on the route, the levante wind can blow for days, and about one coastal morning in four may have cloud.

## Costa del Sol near Málaga

**Why here:** It has the easiest flights and good odds. Atlantic cloud rarely gets past Tarifa, and Marbella scored about 89% in Eclipsophile's survey.

**What to book:** Sleep as far west as you can afford, because totality grows town by town: Málaga 1:48, Torremolinos 2:41, Fuengirola 3:10, Marbella 3:17, Estepona 3:37, Manilva 3:47 (IGN). All fall between 10:46 and 10:50 CEST, with the Sun 39° up.

**Getting there and cost:** Málaga airport is in the path, with 2:16 on NASA's explorer. Reported by [idealista](https://www.idealista.it/news/node/444114), an Estepona flat asked over €4,200 a night, while the Costa del Sol hotel association saw eclipse-date occupancy at some hotels at about 12%, against the usual 7% a year out.

**The trade-off:** It's hotter, with a [30.8°C (87°F)](https://www.aemet.es/es/serviciosclimaticos/datosclimatologicos/valoresclimatologicos?l=6155A&k=and) August high, the whole coast funnels along one road corridor, and Málaga city gets less than half of Tarifa's darkness.

## Ceuta

**Why here:** IGN gives Ceuta Spain's longest totality, 4:48 between 10:45 and 10:50 CEST. [Spain's eclipse commission](https://trioeclipses.es/ccaa/ceuta) names the Royal Walls and Monte Hacho as viewpoints.

**Getting there and cost:** [Aena lists a heliport](https://www.aena.es/en/ceuta.html), not an airport, so you arrive by helicopter or by ferry from Algeciras. [Ferryhopper](https://www.ferryhopper.com/en/ferry-routes/direct/ferry-algeciras-ceuta) shows 1 to 1.5 hours from about €30, in normal times.

**What to book:** The room and both ferry legs together, crossing by 1 August.

**The trade-off:** You can't dodge cloud on a small enclave, and the survey gives about 72%. The US State Department says ["reconsider travel to Ceuta due to unrest"](https://cadataapi.state.gov/api/TravelAdvisories) (1 August 2026), after what [Spain's Interior Ministry](https://www.lamoncloa.gob.es/serviciosdeprensa/notasprensa/interior/Paginas/2026/280826-apoyo-frontex-triaje-ceuta.aspx) calls a migration crisis on 30–31 July. The commission still calls it a premium location, so check again before you pay.

## Melilla

**Why here:** You get 4:34 from 10:48 CEST with the Sun 41° up. [The commission](https://trioeclipses.es/ccaa/melilla) points to the old citadel, Melilla la Vieja.

**Getting there and cost:** Melilla has [its own airport](https://www.aena.es/en/melilla.html). The [ferry from Málaga](https://www.ferryhopper.com/en/ferry-routes/direct/ferry-malaga-melilla) takes 6 to 7 hours, from about €53 in normal times.

**The trade-off:** It has the weakest sky here. Sunshine averages 61% of the possible, against 75–80% at many other places on the track (Eclipsophile). Moving inland would help, but inland is Morocco, across a border.

## Tangier and northern Morocco

**Why here:** The centre line passes about 10 km (6 miles) south of Tangier, so it gets 4:50 and Tetouan 4:51, with the Sun 38° up. Reported by idealista, week-long packages cost much like Djerba's, which start at about €1,200 to €1,300 a person.

**When to go:** Watch the clock. Morocco [moved to GMT all year](https://fnh.ma/article/alaune/maroc-retour-heure-gmt-dimanche-20-septembre-2026) on 20 September 2026 ([tz database](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/eggert/tz/main/africa)), so totality in Tangier is at about 08:45–08:49 local, two hours behind Spain. Read ferry times carefully. Older pages, including Eclipsophile's, still say about 9:44.

**What to book:** Eclipsophile favours central or eastern Tangier and the Bay of Tangier beaches, to avoid lingering mist; the city scored about 84%. Chefchaouen scored about 95% but gets only 4:13. It says to avoid the coast from Tetouan to El Jebha, and cloud can outlast totality at Asilah and Larache.

**Getting there and cost:** Fly to Tangier, which is in the path, or sail from Tarifa to Tanger Ville, [about an hour](https://www.ferryhopper.com/en/ferry-routes/direct/ferry-tarifa-tanger) and about €27 to €40 one way in normal times. Algeciras ferries land at Tanger Med, well outside the city.

**The trade-off:** You need a passport, and you share ports with summer holiday traffic. The UK's [FCDO](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/morocco/safety-and-security) says "terrorists are likely to try to carry out attacks in Morocco" but its [regional advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/morocco/regional-risks) covers only Western Sahara and the closed border with Algeria; the US rates Morocco Level 2.

## Luxor and the Nile

**Why here:** Luxor gets 6:20 and Karnak 6:21, from 13:02 to 13:08 Egyptian summer time (UTC+3), with the Sun 82° up. Eclipsophile says cloud is almost unknown and the main threat is thin cirrus. The centre line, with up to 6:23, passes just north of the city.

**When to go:** Arrive by 1 August and sightsee at dawn. August averages 41.2°C (106°F) by day and 25.6°C (78°F) at night, with 0.07 mm of rain ([NOAA](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/wmo-climate-normals)). Eclipsophile reports an average of 42°C on 2 August over 17 years, with a 46°C record.

**What to book:** A room or cabin for 1 August, now. If Luxor is full, try Sohag (6:22), Qena (6:11), Esna (5:44) or Edfu (5:29). Boats move, so ask where yours will be at 13:00 on 2 August: Aswan is outside the path.

For cooler air, Marsa Alam gets 5:31 with a 35.6°C high. Eclipsophile's safest skies are at Siwa (5:28) and Bahariya (6:17), but the US says to enter the Western Desert only with a licensed tour company.

**Getting there and cost:** Fly into Luxor, usually via Cairo. [Egypt's e-visa portal](https://visa2egypt.gov.eg/eVisa/FAQ) lists US$30 for a single-entry tourist visa (about €27 at the [ECB rate](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/eurofxref/eurofxref-daily.xml) of 6 November 2026).

Reported by [Travel Noire](https://travelnoire.com/2027-total-solar-eclipse-egypt), citing CNN, over 90% of Luxor listings on Booking.com were gone and many of the rest asked over US$2,695 a night (about €2,410). Reported by idealista, a 10-day package from about €4,300 a person has its 30 July departure sold out.

**The trade-off:** You'll stand in 40°C-plus heat with the Sun almost overhead, when even temple columns cast little shade. The [FCDO's warnings](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/egypt) don't cover Luxor, but it says [terrorist attacks are likely in Egypt](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/egypt/safety-and-security), advises against driving outside main cities at night and notes accidents on Nile cruises. Its 27 October update calls the wider Middle East "deeply unpredictable", one more reason to hold a cancellable booking. The US rates Egypt Level 2.

## Tunisia, Libya and Saudi Arabia: what the travel advice says

**Tunisia is worth a look.** Sfax gets 5:39 from 10:09 (UTC+1), with the Sun 56° up and the lowest cloud on the western track (Eclipsophile). The [FCDO](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/tunisia) warns against Mount Chaambi, Kasserine and the Libyan border zone; Sfax and Djerba aren't on its list. But its [safety page](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/tunisia/safety-and-security) says terrorists are likely to try to carry out attacks in Tunisia, lists a 2023 shooting on Djerba, and names Sfax among towns with anti-migrant protests and violence. The US rates Tunisia Level 2 and notes a state of emergency. Fly to Sfax or Djerba: reported by idealista, easyJet bookings to Enfidha jumped, yet Enfidha's airport is outside the path at 99.4%.

**The trade-off:** It's 33°C (92°F) in August, and the resort strip from Hammamet to Monastir misses.

**Libya: no.** The [FCDO](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/libya) advises against all travel, easing to all but essential travel for Tripoli, Benghazi, Al-Bayda, Derna and Misrata. The US says do not travel for any reason.

**Saudi Arabia: not now.** Jeddah gets 5:56, but the [FCDO](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/saudi-arabia) advises against all but essential travel (27 October 2026) and reports a missile strike on Riyadh's main airport on 10 October. The US says reconsider travel and ordered non-emergency staff out on 23 October.

**Algeria, Yemen and Somalia.** Oran gets 5:07. The [FCDO's](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/algeria) Algeria warnings cover border zones, it says terrorists are likely to try to carry out attacks there, and the land border with Morocco is closed. The US rates Yemen and Somalia Level 4, do not travel.

Advice changes, so read it again before you book and fly. Travelling against FCDO advice can invalidate your insurance.

## What the authorities have announced so far

- **Spain:** The [interministerial commission](https://trioeclipses.es/prensa/la-comision-interministerial-del-trio-de-eclipses-inicia-la-preparacion-del-eclipse-del-2-de) began preparing for 2027 on 8 October 2026. [Its mobility page](https://trioeclipses.es/movilidad) lists traffic plans, police reinforcement and a transport study, but we found no 2027 road rules yet. In 2026 the traffic authority, the [DGT](https://www.dgt.es/conoce-el-estado-del-trafico/eclipse-12-de-agosto/index.html), set an "absolute ban" on stopping on carriageways or hard shoulders to watch.
- **Andalusia:** Reported by [El Independiente](https://www.elindependiente.com/espana/andalucia/2026/07/31/andalucia-se-prepara-este-domingo-para-el-eclipse-total-de-2027-con-una-prueba-logistica-inedita/amp/), the region planned a field test of access, parking and services on 2 August 2026; we found no report of the results. Reported by [Moncloa.com](https://www.moncloa.com/2026/08/01/eclipse-solar-2027-andalucia-almeria-3408969/), a news site and not the government, a "mirror day" on 10 May 2027 puts the Sun where it will stand on eclipse day.
- **Gibraltar:** The [government](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/press-releases/planning-begins-for-solar-eclipses-212026-11608) expects visitors, possibly in the thousands, and is planning viewing sites and transport.
- **Egypt:** Reported by [Revista Mercado](https://revistamercado.do/actualidad/el-eclipse-del-siglo-arrancan-los-preparativos-para-el-historico-fenomeno-solar-que-durara-mas-de-6-minutos/), the tourism ministry's committee is coordinating viewing sites, transport and security, and may adjust hours at archaeological sites. We found no published programme.
- **Morocco and Tunisia:** We found no published plan. Reported by [La Presse](https://www.lapresse.tn/?p=4329088), Tunisia's higher education minister chaired the first meeting of an eclipse coordination committee on 16 April 2026.

## What to book first, and how far ahead

[Spain's commission](https://trioeclipses.es/espana-epicentro-mundial-del-turismo-astronomico) warns that rooms and transport in the best areas usually sell out months ahead. So, in this order:

1. **The room, now, nine months out.** Take a free-cancellation rate even at a premium, because your best town may change. Note the deadline and read the terms for those exact nights. It protects you if you change your mind, not if the host does: we found one [public complaint](https://www.ocu.org/reclamar/lista-reclamaciones-publicas/me-cancelan-mi-reserva-ya-conf/448be9a3272d1cc843) about a flat cancelled for 12 August 2026 and, the customer says, resold at higher prices, but no reports of mass host cancellations. We'd still pick a hotel over a private flat. Reported by Andalucía Informa, tourist rentals in Andalusia must be on a national register, so ask for the number.
2. **Don't pay five figures in panic.** Reported by idealista in September 2026, occupancy for the eclipse dates at the rental firm Ruralidays had reached only 12% in Cádiz province, and 22% in El Gastor, which gets no totality. Reported by Euronews, some Cádiz hotels have temporarily closed bookings. Keep checking through the winter, and go non-refundable only at a price you could afford to lose.
3. **Flights, within weeks.** Airlines usually sell about a year ahead, so August 2027 is on sale or close to it. Arrive by 31 July and leave on 3 August or later.
4. **A car, with the flights.** Take a cancellable rate, collect it on arrival, and check the contract before putting it on a ferry.
5. **Ferries, as soon as sailings appear.** We found no announcement of extra eclipse crossings. [Spain's Strait holiday-traffic operation](https://www.lamoncloa.gob.es/serviciosdeprensa/notasprensa/interior/paginas/2026/160726-balance-operacion-paso-estrecho.aspx) ran from 15 June to 15 September in 2026; the 2027 dates aren't out. Cross by 1 August, never on eclipse morning.
6. **Glasses, by spring.** Spain gave away 2 million pairs in 2026; we found no 2027 giveaway announced.
7. **Paperwork.** The [EU's ETIAS](https://travel-europe.europa.eu/en/etias) authorisation for visa-exempt visitors will cost €20, with the start date to be announced several months ahead. If you need a Schengen visa, start early.

## On the day: where to stand, roads, heat and water

**Where to stand.** In Spain and Morocco the Sun is 37–41° up, almost due east, so pick open ground with nothing tall to the east. In Luxor it's almost overhead, so any open ground works. Check Andalusia's official viewing points on the commission's site closer to the date.

**Getting there.** The commission asks you to avoid travel on the day, "especially after the maximum", and says a spot near a main road with about a minute of totality is fully satisfying. We'd sleep inside the path and walk. If you must drive, park by 09:00 CEST: the partial phase starts at about 09:41 (07:40 in Tangier, 11:40 in Luxor).

**Leaving.** The [2026 report](https://www.ciencia.gob.es/dam/jcr:74bd4067-d37a-44d1-b42e-bba1d843cafa/Resumen%20Ejecutivo%20Actuaciones%20Comisi%C3%B3n%20Interministerial%20Tr%C3%ADo%20Eclipses%202026.pdf) counted 26 million extra trips nationwide that day, on foot included, and 64% of same-day departures from the totality band came between 21:00 and 22:00, just after the eclipse. National roads saw only localised jams, but the DGT warned about secondary roads and exits from viewing points. Stay for lunch and leave in late afternoon.

**Heat and water.** Bring water, food, a full tank and a charged phone ([the commission's checklist](https://trioeclipses.es/sites/default/files/2026-07/infografia-decalogo-turismo-responsable-eclipse.pdf)), and download offline maps, because [networks may saturate](https://trioeclipses.es/prevencion-y-riesgos). In Luxor, Eclipsophile suggests a buddy system for heat stroke and a damp white cloth over your camera, since many shut down near 45°C.

**Fire.** The commission says the eclipse falls in Spain's peak fire season: no flames, and no parking on dry vegetation.

## Eye safety: glasses, phones and the only safe moment

- **Glasses:** Use viewers that meet ISO 12312-2, as [NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/eclipses/safety/) and the AAS both advise. [In the EU](https://trioeclipses.es/salud), look for "EN ISO 12312-2:2015" and a genuine CE mark. "ISO 12312-1" means ordinary sunglasses, and those aren't safe, however dark.
- **Where to buy:** The AAS says to buy from known makers or [listed suppliers](https://eclipse.aas.org/eye-safety/viewers-filters), not from whatever pops up in ads; for Europe it points to Spain's Eclipse Seguro list. Spain's 2026 inspections flagged 7 models, too dark rather than dangerous, and found more failures online than in shops.
- **When to take them off:** Only when the Moon completely covers the Sun's bright face. Put them back as soon as the bright Sun returns (AAS). [Spain's commission](https://trioeclipses.es/salud) agrees that's the only safe moment, though it recommends leaving them on. Outside the path, that moment never comes.
- **Cameras, binoculars and telescopes:** Never use eclipse glasses with them. NASA and the [AAS](https://eclipse.aas.org/eye-safety/optics-filters) say the solar filter must be fixed to the front of the optics.
- **Phones:** [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/science-research/five-tips-from-nasa-for-photographing-a-total-solar-eclipse/) says to protect any camera with a solar filter during the partial phases and remove it for totality. Neither NASA nor the AAS gives phone settings, though the AAS lists smartphone filters.
- **Before you go:** Throw away scratched or torn viewers (AAS) and supervise children (the commission). [ESA](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/science/a-look-back-at-the-2026-total-solar-eclipse) suggests a pinhole camera as a safe indirect view.

## Sample plans: five days in Cádiz, seven days on the Nile

**Five days in Cádiz province.** Land at Jerez on Saturday 31 July, collect a car and settle around Vejer or Benalup. On Sunday, test your viewing spot at 10:45, then swim at El Palmar. On Monday be in place by 09:00: the partial phase starts at about 09:41, totality comes at about 10:45 and it ends around noon, so stay put for a long lunch. Tuesday is for Tarifa and the Roman ruins at Bolonia, Wednesday for Cádiz city before you fly. [Plan the days around it](/trips/new) and adjust them by chatting.

**Seven days on the Nile and in Luxor.** Fly via Cairo to Aswan on Thursday 29 July and join a boat sailing north past Kom Ombo and Edfu, reaching Luxor by Sunday 1 August; check its calendar first, because Aswan is outside the path. On Monday 2 August the partial phase starts at 11:40, totality runs from 13:02 to 13:08 and it ends at 14:26, so bring shade and plenty of water. Spend Tuesday's dawn on the West Bank and Wednesday's at Karnak, then fly home that day. [Build your itinerary](/trips/new) with the eclipse on day five.

## Frequently asked questions

### What time is the eclipse on 2 August 2027?

Totality starts at about 10:45 to 10:50 CEST in Spain, 08:45 GMT in northern Morocco, 10:09 (UTC+1) in Sfax and 13:02 (UTC+3) in Luxor.

### Where can I see totality in Spain?

In Ceuta, Melilla, almost all of Cádiz province, much of Málaga province and the southern edges of Granada and Almería. Everywhere else in Spain gets a partial eclipse of at least 70%, says IGN.

### Will Málaga, Seville and Granada see totality?

Málaga will, for 1:48. Seville and Granada won't: they reach 98.4% and 99.2%. From Granada, the coast at Motril or Almuñécar is the nearest totality.

### Can I see the total eclipse from Italy?

No. Lampedusa comes closest at 99.8%. Palermo gets about 90%, Rome 75% and Milan 62%. The nearest totality on land is in Tunisia.

### Can I see it from the US, Brazil or India?

Not from Brazil, and from the US only a tiny nick at sunrise in eastern Maine. India gets a late-afternoon partial eclipse: Bengaluru 39%, Mumbai 32%, Delhi 8%, all around 16:30 to 17:00 local time.

### Spain or Egypt: which is better?

Egypt wins on length and sky, with 6:20 and almost no cloud. Spain wins on ease and comfort: a short flight for Europeans, mid-morning timing and coastal highs of 25–31°C.

### Is it too late to book?

No, but start now. Luxor looks tightest. In Spain, listings are expensive, but the press reports low occupancy so far. Hold a cancellable room and keep looking.

### When is the next total eclipse in Spain?

Not until 2053, says IGN. The next solar eclipse visible from Spain is the annular one on 26 January 2028.
