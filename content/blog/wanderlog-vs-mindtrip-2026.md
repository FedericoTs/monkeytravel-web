These two tools get recommended in the same breath, and they should not be. They solve opposite halves of the problem.

Wanderlog assumes you have already decided. Mindtrip assumes you have not. If you pick the wrong one for where you are in your planning, you will spend an evening fighting the interface instead of choosing a hotel.

Here is what each is genuinely good at, what they cost in 2026, and the thing neither of them does.

## The short version

**Wanderlog** is a very good organiser with planning features bolted on. You already know you are going to Kyoto. You have a list of eleven places from Instagram and a friend's spreadsheet. Wanderlog turns that pile into a map, a route, and a day-by-day structure.

**Mindtrip** is a chat-driven generator. You do not know where you are going, or you know the city but not the shape of the trip. You describe what you want and it produces something.

The dividing line is whether the research is done. Organiser or generator. Almost every complaint you read about either tool is someone using it for the other job.

## Pricing, plainly

| | Free tier | Paid |
|---|---|---|
| **Wanderlog** | Pins, days, collaboration, manual budgets | **Pro $39.99/yr** — offline access, AI assistant, route optimisation, booking deals, unlimited attachments, Google Maps export, Gmail scanning, dark mode |
| **Mindtrip** | Core planning is free | Booking is the business model rather than a subscription |

Two things worth noticing in that table.

First, Wanderlog puts **route optimisation behind the paywall**. That is the single most useful thing the product does, and it is the reason most people eventually pay the $39.99. Fair enough, but know that going in: the free tier is the notepad, not the optimiser.

Second, Mindtrip's free tier is genuinely free in the sense that matters, but as of mid-2026 the planner sits behind a Google or Apple login wall. You create an account before you see a single itinerary. And the flow is built to steer toward booking inside the app, which is where the money is.

## Where Wanderlog wins

**Multi-stop logistics.** If your trip is Rome, then Florence, then Venice, Wanderlog is the strongest of the two by a distance. The route optimiser visibly shows you when you are backtracking, which is the mistake that quietly eats a day of a two-week trip. If that is the trip you are planning, our guide to [planning a multi-city trip](/blog/how-to-plan-a-multi-city-trip) covers the sequencing decisions no tool makes for you.

**Collaboration.** Shared trips work properly. Several people can add places, and the map updates for everyone. If you are the person in the group chat who ends up owning the plan, this is the feature you want.

**Import from the mess.** Gmail scanning on Pro pulls confirmations in automatically. It is unglamorous and it is the feature people keep paying for.

## Where Mindtrip wins

**Starting from nothing.** Ask it a vague question and you get something concrete back. That is a real capability and Wanderlog does not have it in the same way.

**Screenshot context.** You can hand it a screenshot from social media and it will map the places in it. This is genuinely clever, and it closes the gap between "I saved this reel" and "I know where that is."

**Community guides.** There is real curated content underneath, which makes the suggestions less generic than a raw model output.

## What neither of them does well

Both tools give you a list of places and a map. Neither reliably hands you a **day you can actually walk**.

That sounds like a small distinction. It is not. A good day has three or four stops that are near each other, in an order that makes geographic sense, with realistic gaps for eating and getting between them. A bad day has five excellent places scattered across a city and no acknowledgement that you will spend two hours in transit.

The other shared gap is the account wall. Mindtrip asks you to log in before you see anything. Wanderlog lets you look, then gates the useful part. In both cases you commit before you know whether the output is any good.

## So which one

**Choose Wanderlog** if the research is done and the problem is order, logistics and other people. Budget for the $39.99 — the free tier is not the product people rave about.

**Choose Mindtrip** if you are starting cold and want something to react to, and you do not mind creating an account first.

**Choose neither** if what you actually want is a finished schedule you can read before signing up for anything. That is the gap both leave open, and it is the one we built for.

Whichever you pick, do the same sanity check on the output: take day one and trace the stops on a map. If the route zigzags across the city, the tool gave you a list and called it a plan.

## What changed in 2026

Both tools moved, and in opposite directions.

Wanderlog pushed further into **paid utility**. The Pro tier picked up offline access and Gmail scanning, which makes it more valuable to frequent travellers and less useful on the free tier than it was two years ago. It is becoming a subscription product that happens to have a free entry point.

Mindtrip pushed further into **booking**. The login requirement and the in-app booking flow are not accidents; they are the business model becoming visible. Expect the gap between "plan here" and "book here" to keep narrowing.

The practical consequence for you: Wanderlog's answer to "how do we make money" is your $39.99. Mindtrip's answer is a commission on your hotel. Neither is wrong, but they pull the products in different directions, and it shows up in what each one nudges you toward.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Wanderlog free?**
There is a real free tier covering pins, days, collaboration and manual budgets. Route optimisation, offline access, Gmail scanning and Google Maps export are Pro, at $39.99 per year. Most people who recommend Wanderlog are describing Pro.

**Does Mindtrip require an account?**
Yes. As of mid-2026 the planner sits behind a Google or Apple login, so you create an account before seeing an itinerary. Core planning is free once you are in.

**Which is better for a multi-city trip?**
Wanderlog, clearly. The route optimiser shows you when you are backtracking between cities, which is the error that quietly costs a day on a two-week trip. Mindtrip is stronger at deciding where to go, weaker at sequencing once you know.

**Which is better for group trips?**
Wanderlog. Shared trips work properly, several people can add places, and the map updates for everyone. Mindtrip is built around a single planner's conversation.

**Can either one book for me?**
Mindtrip is built to, and steers you toward booking in-app. Wanderlog surfaces booking deals on Pro but is primarily a planning tool. If a single plan-and-book flow matters, that favours Mindtrip.

**What if I do not want an account at all?**
Then neither fits cleanly. Mindtrip requires one up front, and Wanderlog gates its best feature behind a subscription. Tools that show a full itinerary with no account are a short list, ours included.
