# Camino de Santiago 2027: Holy Year Routes, Crowds, Rules and Beds

The Holy Year (Año Santo, or Xacobeo in Galician) runs from 31 December 2026 to 31 December 2027. No change to the Compostela rules has been announced, no authority has published a pilgrim forecast, and the Camino is at a record before the Holy Door even opens: 562,115 pilgrims by 5 November 2026, more than in all of 2025.

This guide is for anyone walking or cycling in 2027 who needs to pick a route and a month and know what to book first. One surprise in the official numbers: May and September are now busier than July.

Every date and rule was read on the official page on 6 November 2026. Figures from the press or independent guides are marked. Times are Spanish local time (CET in winter, CEST in summer); Portugal is one hour behind.

## What is the 2027 Holy Year, and why is 2027 one?

Santiago holds a Holy Year whenever 25 July, the feast of St James, falls on a Sunday, as it does in 2027 ([Cathedral](https://catedraldesantiago.es/peregrinacion/)). The last began in 2021 and was [extended through 2022](https://catedraldesantiago.es/el-papa-concede-prorrogar-el-ano-jubilar-compostelano-durante-todo-2022/) because of the pandemic. The next after 2027 is [2032](https://www.santiagoturismo.com/historia/cando-e-ano-santo). Italians call the route the Cammino di Santiago; Portuguese and Brazilians, the Caminho de Santiago.

A Holy Year changes the cathedral more than the path. The Holy Door (Puerta Santa) on Praza da Quintana opens for twelve months, pilgrims [traditionally enter through it](https://www.caminodesantiago.gal/es/descubre/origenes-y-evolucion/el-ano-santo-compostelano), and the Church offers a plenary indulgence. [Santiago's tourist office](https://www.santiagoturismo.com/compostela2027) gives 31 December 2026 to 31 December 2027 as the official period.

## When does the Holy Door open?

The date is agreed. The hour is not. On 24 September the cathedral's dean said the opening on 31 December 2026 moves to around noon instead of the usual 17:00 (reported by [El Correo Gallego](https://www.elcorreogallego.es/santiago/2026/09/24/santiago-prepara-ano-santo-xacobeo-2027-134639233.html), [COPE](https://www.cope.es/emisoras/galicia/a-coruna-provincia/santiago/noticias/ano-santo-27-madruga-puerta-santa-abrira-31-diciembre-mediodia-20260924_3443167.html) and [Religión Digital](https://www.religiondigital.org/diocesis/puerta-santa-catedral-santiago-adelantara-xacobeo-2027_1_1467526.html)).

On 6 November the cathedral's own [liturgical calendar](https://catedraldesantiago.es/liturgia/) still read: "A las 17:00 h apertura solemne de la Puerta Santa." The [Archdiocese's note](https://archicompostela.org/una-estrella-para-nuestro-tiempo-el-arzobispo-situa-la-alegria-de-caminar-con-esperanza-en-el-centro-del-ano-santo-compostelano-2027-bajo-el-lema-al-ver-la-estrella-se-llenaron-de-inmensa-a/) on the 24 September event gives no time. So plan for noon, keep the whole day free and recheck the cathedral page in December. The last opening, in 2020, [began at 16:30](https://catedraldesantiago.es/ceremonia-de-apertura-de-la-puerta-santa-e-inicio-del-ano-jubilar-compostelano-2021/).

The closing hour on 31 December 2027 is not announced. In 2022 the cathedral [announced the closing ceremony for 16:30, with entry through the door cut at 15:00](https://catedraldesantiago.es/ceremonia-de-clausura-del-ano-santo-compostelano-2021-2022/), so don't plan a last-minute arrival.

## What changes for a walker in 2027?

- **The rules:** nothing announced. The [Compostela page](https://oficinadelperegrino.com/peregrinacion/la-compostela/) doesn't mention 2027.
- **Groups:** the European Youth Pilgrimage brings people aged 16 to 35 to Santiago on [15–18 July 2027](https://archicompostela.org/encuentro-del-arzobispo-con-los-responsables-de-la-organizacion-de-la-pej-2027/), on [13 routes](https://archicompostela.org/la-delegacion-de-juventud-de-santiago-lanza-la-web-oficial-de-la-peregrinacion-europea-de-jovenes-2027/) including those from Sarria, Tui, Ferrol and Lugo. Expect the days before it to be crowded.
- **Public beds:** still first come, first served. In October, wardens on the Camino Portugués asked for bookings at peak times, above all for groups, and the decision rests with the Xunta (reported by [moncloa.com](https://www.moncloa.com/2026/10/05/xacobeo-2027-albergues-camino-portugues-pontevedra-3442675/)).
- **Pilgrim Mass:** works at the high altar [moved it to the church of San Agustín](https://catedraldesantiago.es/traslado-temporal-de-las-misas-del-peregrino-a-la-iglesia-de-san-agustin/) from 16 October 2026 until further notice, at 9:30, 12:00 and 19:30. The 7:30 Pilgrim Mass stays in the cathedral, in the Communion chapel. There are [daily Masses in Italian](https://catedraldesantiago.es/liturgia/) (16:30, Santa María do Camiño) and English (10:30, Pilgrim's Office chapel, not Wednesdays).
- **Backpacks:** [not allowed in the cathedral](https://catedraldesantiago.es/liturgia/). Correos has a [left-luggage office](https://www.elcaminoconcorreos.com/en/luggage-storage) two minutes from the Obradoiro.

These monthly figures are the dashboard's chart, which leaves out a few records with no route, so the 2025 and 2026 columns add up to 530,713 and 562,085.

**Busiest:** September, August, May and June. September 2026 passed the [85,755 of August 2022](https://www.diariodesantiago.es/santiago/el-camino-afronta-el-xacobeo-2027-con-un-nuevo-record-de-peregrinos-en-el-horizonte/), the record until then.

**Quietest:** January and February, then December, November and March. The office saw about 80 pilgrims a day in January 2026 and about 3,000 a day that September.

The advice to go in May or September for more room is out of date: both now beat July, and October 2026 did too. The Xunta itself says [mid and low seasons grew 28%](https://www.xunta.gal/es/notas-de-prensa/-/nova/026443/xunta-refuerza-control-flujos-catedral-santiago-desestacionalizacion-del-camino).

March won't be as quiet as the table suggests, because Easter is 28 March 2027: March 2024, with Easter on the 31st, was double March 2023. Holy Years lift the quiet months too. December 2010 was almost six times December 2009, though from a tiny base.

The trail is fuller than these counts, because the Xunta's sensors [find 1.68 walkers per Compostela](https://www.xunta.gal/es/notas-de-prensa/-/nova/026443/xunta-refuerza-control-flujos-catedral-santiago-desestacionalizacion-del-camino). And the last 100 km carry the load: Sarria alone had 28,208 starters in September 2026, about 940 a day.

By route in 2025: Francés 241,996, Portugués 100,803, Portugués coast 89,503, Inglés 30,200, Primitivo 27,866, Norte 21,481. The Francés fell from 45.6% of pilgrims in 2025 to 42.9% in 2026, while the two Portuguese routes rose from 35.9% to 39.7%. So far in 2026, Spain has sent 38% of pilgrims, the US 9% and Italy 8%.

**The forecast for 2027:** no authority has published a number. The Xunta writes only of an [expected rise](https://www.caminodesantiago.gal/es/sala-de-prensa/detalle?content=/60-Espazo-institucional/.content/noticias/noticia_1854.xml), and the Holy Year commissioner says they [hope to keep growing](https://www.elespanol.com/eventos/2026/v-foro-economico-galicia/20260616/potencial-xacobeo-personas-hacen-camino-vienen/1003744286481_0.html) (reported by El Español). The 730,000 you may see quoted is a sensor estimate for 2026 so far (reported by [moncloa.com](https://www.moncloa.com/2026/09/26/xacobeo-2027-rueda-campana-madrid-3438242/)), not a forecast.

**25 July:** the high point, on a Sunday. The 2027 timetable isn't out. Our [2027 travel calendar](/blog/2027-travel-calendar) shows what else fills that week.

## Which Camino route for how many days?

Start from the [days you can spare](/blog/how-long-should-a-trip-be), at about [25 km a day](https://oficinadelperegrino.com/preguntas-frecuentes/), the Pilgrim's Office average. Every route here qualifies for the Compostela except the last.

### Camino Francés from Sarria: 6 days

**Why here:** the shortest way to the Compostela on the route with the most services. The [Xunta's six stages](https://www.caminodesantiago.gal/es/planifica/las-rutas/camino-frances) add up to 114.8 km.

**When to go:** early March or November for space. Avoid mid-July, August and September if crowds bother you.

**What to book:** private beds in Portomarín, Palas de Rei, Melide, Arzúa and O Pedrouzo as soon as your dates are fixed.

**Getting there and cost:** a direct bus from Santiago takes 2 to 2¾ hours and a train from Madrid 4 to 5 hours (tour operator [Santiago Ways](https://santiagoways.com/en/how-to-get-to-sarria/)). The public bed is €10; private albergues in Sarria [charge €12–16](https://www.gronze.com/etapa/sarria/portomarin) (Gronze).

**The trade-off:** this is the crowd. Three in ten pilgrims start here, you will rarely walk alone, and Gronze warns that [albergues fill up in Galicia](https://www.gronze.com/camino-de-santiago/consejos) in July and August.

### Camino Francés from León: 13 days

**Why here:** about 305 km of real variety, over the mountains at Foncebadón and up to O Cebreiro, where Galicia begins.

**When to go:** spring or early autumn. León's January lows [average below freezing](https://www.aemet.es/en/serviciosclimaticos/datosclimatologicos/valoresclimatologicos?k=cle&l=2661).

**The trade-off:** two hard mountain days, and the last five days merge with the Sarria crowd.

### Camino Francés from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port: a month

**Why here:** the classic full route, [773 km in 31 sections](https://www.elcaminoconcorreos.com/en/camino-frances-the-french-way) by Correos's count, about 764 km by [Gronze's stages](https://www.gronze.com/camino-frances).

**When to go:** April to October. The high Napoleon route over Lepoeder closes from 1 November to 31 March, leaving only Valcarlos (reported by tour operator [Viaje Camino de Santiago](https://viajecaminodesantiago.com/consejos-peregrinos/cierre-invernal-ruta-napoleon-valcarlos/)).

**The trade-off:** a month off, a first day Gronze rates 5 out of 5, and the same Sarria crowd in the final week.

### Camino Portugués from Tui: 6 days

**Why here:** the gentlest short option at [118.8 km](https://www.caminodesantiago.gal/es/planifica/las-rutas/camino-portugues), rated medium-low by the Xunta.

**What to book:** private beds in Pontevedra and Caldas de Reis, where a public bed is already a lottery in high season (reported by moncloa.com).

**Getting there and cost:** Tui has [no passenger trains](https://santiagoways.com/en/how-to-get-to-tui/). The nearest station is Guillarei, a few kilometres away, or take a bus from Vigo (Santiago Ways).

**The trade-off:** ground that's [mainly asphalt or dirt tracks](https://www.elcaminoconcorreos.com/en/camino-portugues-the-portuguese-way) (Correos), and a crowd that grows every year.

### Camino Portugués from Porto, central route: 12 days

**Why here:** two countries in about 245 km (Correos rounds it to 250 km), with cheaper beds in Portugal: [€5 at Ponte de Lima's pilgrim albergue](https://www.gronze.com/etapa/barcelos/ponte-lima) (Gronze).

**Getting there and cost:** Metro line E runs from Porto's airport to the city, and ALSA buses link Santiago with Porto airport in [about 4 hours](https://www.gronze.com/camino-de-santiago/como-llegar/porto) (Gronze).

**The trade-off:** a 34.5 km stage from Barcelos to Ponte de Lima unless you split it, and from Tui you share the path with everyone who started there.

### Camino Portugués from Porto, coast: 13 days

**Why here:** the sea for much of the way and about 270 km with no big climbs. The [Galician part from A Guarda](https://www.caminodesantiago.gal/es/planifica/las-rutas/camino-portugues-de-la-costa) is 163.1 km.

**When to go:** April to October. The [subsidised Xacobeo Transfer boat from Caminha to A Guarda](https://www.gronze.com/etapa/caminha/porto-mougas/al-loro) runs 7:00–15:30 Portuguese time in that season but only 8:00–9:00 from November to March, and cost €9 in 2026. Private boats also cross (Gronze).

**The trade-off:** it's no longer the quiet choice. The Xunta reports 22% growth in early 2026, and the last 85 km are shared with the central route.

### Camino Inglés from Ferrol: 5 days

**Why here:** [112.4 km](https://www.caminodesantiago.gal/es/planifica/las-rutas/camino-ingles) (Correos says 122 km), with under a fifth of Sarria's numbers.

**What to book:** a private bed in Betanzos. Its public albergue [closed for works in October 2026](https://www.caminodesantiago.gal/es/sala-de-prensa/detalle?content=/60-Espazo-institucional/.content/noticias/noticia_1854.xml), and the Xunta gives no reopening date.

**Getting there and cost:** buses from Santiago airport are limited, and from A Coruña airport you change in the city for Monbus or Renfe ([Viaje Camino de Santiago](https://viajecaminodesantiago.com/en/pilgrims-useful-information/how-to-get-to-ferrol/)).

**The trade-off:** the Xunta's [Miño to Bruma stage](https://www.caminodesantiago.gal/es/planifica/las-rutas/camino-ingles/etapa-mino-bruma) is 34.7 km and rated hard. The shorter start from A Coruña is 72.8 km, which only counts if you began on foot outside Spain.

### Camino Primitivo from Oviedo: 14 days

**Why here:** mountains and fewer people. Correos gives 323 km, [Spain's tourist board](https://www.spain.info/en/camino-santiago/primitive-way-camino-santiago/) 325 km, and Gronze's stages about 306 km.

**The trade-off:** Spain's tourist board calls it possibly the most demanding route, though the [Xunta rates its Galician part low](https://www.caminodesantiago.gal/es/planifica/las-rutas/camino-primitivo), so Asturias is the hard bit. You join the Francés at Melide for the last 53 km.

### Camino del Norte from Irún: 5 weeks

**Why here:** the Cantabrian coast with few people: 5,364 started at Irún in 2025. Correos gives 820 km, [Spain's tourist board](https://www.spain.info/en/camino-santiago/northern-way-camino-coast/) 825 km, in about 35 stages.

**The trade-off:** five weeks, hard going in inland Bizkaia, [built-up stretches](https://www.gronze.com/camino-norte) (Gronze), and the [last 39 km shared with the Francés](https://www.caminodesantiago.gal/es/planifica/las-rutas/camino-del-norte) from Arzúa.

### Finisterre and Muxía from Santiago: 3 to 5 days

**Why here:** the walk on to the Atlantic. The [Xunta's stages](https://www.caminodesantiago.gal/es/planifica/las-rutas/camino-de-fisterra-e-muxia) add up to about 90 km to Fisterra and about 88 km to Muxía, with 28.7 km from Fisterra to Muxía and 32 km the other way.

**Getting there and cost:** Monbus runs back to Santiago [3 to 5 times a day](https://www.gronze.com/camino-de-santiago/como-llegar/santiago-finisterre) in 2 to 3 hours (Gronze).

**The trade-off:** no Compostela, since the rules require walking towards Santiago. The two towns [issue their own certificates](https://viajecaminodesantiago.com/en/pilgrims-useful-information/muxiana-fisterrana/) (Viaje Camino de Santiago). The Negreira to Olveiroa stage is 34.3 km.

## Compostela rules: distances, credential and stamps

From the [Pilgrim's Office](https://oficinadelperegrino.com/peregrinacion/la-compostela/), with no change announced for 2027.

- **On foot or horseback:** at least 100 continuous km on one route towards Santiago, ending at the cathedral.
- **By bike:** at least 200 km on the same conditions.
- **By sail:** 100 nautical miles, then on foot from the port. Only the Spanish version of the page lists this.
- **If you started on foot outside Spain:** at least 70 km inside Spain on a recognised route.
- **Credential:** the [official one or one the cathedral authorises](https://oficinadelperegrino.com/preparacion/planificacion/), from the office, associations, parishes or albergues. It has no expiry date, which the office explains as meaning you can collect the Compostela later, in person, and you [need it for public albergues](https://www.caminodesantiago.gal/es/descubre/el-camino-en-la-actualidad/como-hacer-el-camino).
- **Stamps:** at least two a day, dated. The [FAQ](https://oficinadelperegrino.com/preguntas-frecuentes/) applies this at least to the last 100 km (200 by bike). Churches, albergues, hostels and town halls all stamp.
- **Sections:** fine in order, but if you do only the minimum, stamp at the start and end of each section.
- **What doesn't count:** mixing routes, walking away from Santiago, or other transport.
- **Also:** it's free, given only in person, and worded for a Christian purpose. The [credential](https://oficinadelperegrino.com/peregrinacion/la-credencial/) says an attitude of search is enough.

**The Holy Year indulgence, for those who want it:** [visit the tomb in the cathedral and pray](https://catedraldesantiago.es/peregrinacion/), for example an Our Father and the Creed for the Pope's intentions. Go to confession within 15 days before or after, and take communion. It can be gained once a day, by any means of travel, and is separate from the Compostela.

Religión Digital reports that entering through the Holy Door is also required. The cathedral's and the [Pilgrim's Office's](https://oficinadelperegrino.com/peregrinacion/indulgencia-plenaria/) lists don't say so, but using the door costs nothing. The two lists do differ on one point: the Pilgrim's Office also asks you to take part in a Mass or other service during the visit, and the cathedral doesn't.

## How albergues work, and where to sleep in Santiago

The [Xunta's public network](https://www.caminodesantiago.gal/es/descubre/el-camino-en-la-actualidad/la-red-de-albergues-publicos) has 76 albergues and over 3,000 beds in Galicia. A bed costs €10, and you can't book under any circumstances. Doors open 13:00–22:00, you stay one night and leave by 8:00. Walkers get priority over cyclists, and pilgrims with physical limitations over everyone.

[Most private albergues take bookings](https://www.gronze.com/camino-de-santiago/albergues-peregrinos) (Gronze). Gronze's listings on 6 November showed dorm beds at [€14–17 in O Pedrouzo](https://www.gronze.com/etapa/pedrouzo-pino/santiago-compostela) and private rooms from roughly €30 to €60 along the way.

In Santiago the cheapest beds are outside the centre: €10 at Monte do Gozo and at San Lázaro, 3.3 km from the cathedral. The Seminario Menor, 1 km out, charges €20–25, about what old-town albergues start at (Gronze). Santiago also charges a tourist tax of €1 to €2.50 a night for up to five nights, but not in the Xunta's albergues (reported by [Viaje Camino de Santiago](https://viajecaminodesantiago.com/en/news/tourist-tax-in-galicia-and-the-camino-de-santiago/)).

## Flights to Santiago and Porto

Santiago airport lost [routes Ryanair dropped](https://www.aena.es/doc/detalleprensa/251023-np-reunion-lucena-rueda-galicia.pdf), per Aena, and is gaining others. Wizz Air opens a base on 1 February 2027, with Rome from 14 December 2026 (reported by [Forbes](https://forbes.es/forbestravel/992762/el-aeropuerto-rosalia-de-castro-amplia-su-mapa-wizz-air-conectara-santiago-con-diez-nuevos-destinos/)). Fly2Galicia plans Milan, Venice and other routes from 1 December through an unnamed operator (reported by [Euronews](https://es.euronews.com/2026/08/18/una-nueva-aerolinea-de-bajo-coste-lanza-vuelos-directos-a-santiago-de-compostela-en-espana)), so check they are flying before you rely on them. For the Portuguese routes, fly to Porto.

## Luggage transfer and what a day costs

Correos's [Paq Mochila](https://www.elcaminoconcorreos.com/en/rucksack-transfer) moves one bag of up to 15 kg (33 lb) up to 60 km a day. Book by 20:00 the day before, leave the bag by 8:00, and it arrives by 14:30. You need a booking at the next place or its permission, so ask first at public albergues. Last winter it ran only from Sarria and Tui.

Its price appears only in the booking form, and an independent guide estimates [€5–7 a day](https://stingynomads.com/camino-frances-cost/). Gronze also [lists JacoTrans, Caminofácil and Pilbeo](https://www.gronze.com/camino-de-santiago/servicios/transporte-mochilas).

No authority publishes a daily cost. Independent guides put a lean day at [€30–40 and a comfortable one at €60–100 or more](https://www.thebudgetsavvytravelers.com/how-much-does-it-cost-to-walk-the-camino-de-santiago-2026-budget-guide/). A survey of 2,000 people on the Francés, used by the Camino's association of municipalities, found [84% of pilgrims spent under €50 a day](https://www.alberguescaminosantiago.com/2023/03/perfil-de-los-peregrinos-y-turistas-del-camino-de-santiago/) (reported in 2023). Treat these as estimates, as Holy Year prices aren't known.

## Winter and safety

Winter is the quiet you pay for. [Some albergues and services close](https://www.gronze.com/camino-de-santiago/consejos) (Gronze), and an independent site keeps a [list of year-round albergues on the Francés](https://www.alberguescaminosantiago.com/camino-frances/albergues-abiertos-todo-el-ano/). Call a day or two ahead.

The [Guardia Civil](https://web.guardiacivil.es/es/colaboracion/consejos/Camino_de_Santiago/index.html) advises walking in daylight and carrying ID. It also says to avoid walking alone, above all if you're older, "and, if possible, between the months of November and March" (our translation). The Guardián function in the Interior Ministry's [AlertCops app](https://alertcops.ses.mir.es/publico/alertcops/) shares your last 10 positions with a 24-hour centre. Emergencies are 112, and the Guardia Civil is 062. The Xunta publishes an [emergency guide](https://www.caminodesantiago.gal/documents/17639/100607/Folleto_guia_peregrinos_E_V6.pdf?version=1.0), and our [solo travel guide](/blog/solo-travel-planning-with-ai) covers planning alone.

## The Pilgrim's Office queue

The [office](https://oficinadelperegrino.com/oficina-peregrino/) at Rúa das Carretas 33 opens 10:00–19:00 daily. It closes on 25 December and 1 January, when the cathedral sacristy issues the Compostela instead.

[Register by QR code](https://oficinadelperegrino.com/peregrinacion/la-compostela/) at the office or on its [online form](https://oficinadelperegrino.com/registro-individual/), and write the code you receive on your credential. Then take a numbered ticket, one per person. Its QR code shows the queue live, so you can leave and return at least 50 numbers before yours. At peak times the office doesn't guarantee same-day collection, so keep a spare night.

## Sample plans: 6 days from Sarria, 12 days from Porto

**Six days from Sarria (114.8 km).** Sleep in Sarria the night before and get your first stamp there. Walk to Portomarín (22 km), Palas de Rei (25.1 km), Melide (14.6 km), Arzúa (14.5 km), O Pedrouzo (18.4 km) and Santiago (20.2 km), all Xunta stages. Collect two stamps a day, book private beds from April to October, and keep a spare night in Santiago for the queue and the Holy Door. You can [lay these stages out day by day](/trips/new) in the planner, adjust them by chatting and share the plan; it doesn't book beds.

**Twelve days from Porto (about 245 km).** Walk to Vilarinho (26.5 km) and Barcelos (28.1 km), then split the 34.5 km to Ponte de Lima with a night at Vitorino dos Piães, which has beds on the trail. Continue to Rubiães (17.4 km) and Tui (19.1 km), where clocks go forward an hour. In Spain, walk to O Porriño (18.1 km), Redondela (16.4 km), Pontevedra (18.2 km), Caldas de Reis (22.9 km), Padrón (18.8 km) and Santiago (25.2 km). Gronze's stages to Tui, the Xunta's after. The coast takes about a day longer. [Build your itinerary](/trips/new) from these stops and swap towns to suit your pace.

## What to book first, and how far ahead

No authority sets booking windows, so this is our advice.

1. **Now:** choose your route and month, and book Santiago if you want 24–25 July, 15–18 July or 31 December.
2. **6 to 9 months ahead, for April to October:** private beds on the last stages and your Santiago nights, plus one spare night. For other months, 2 to 3 months is enough.
3. **As soon as schedules open:** flights and long-distance trains. Keep a free day before your first stage.
4. **Two months ahead:** start training, as the [Xunta advises](https://www.caminodesantiago.gal/es/descubre/el-camino-en-la-actualidad/como-hacer-el-camino) if you're sedentary, and get your credential from an association. You can also collect one on arrival.
5. **Up to the night before:** bag transfers, which Correos takes until 20:00.

## Frequently asked questions

### When does the 2027 Holy Year start and end?

From 31 December 2026 to 31 December 2027, according to Santiago's tourist office. The opening hour is unresolved: around noon according to the dean, 17:00 on the cathedral's calendar.

### Is the Compostela different in a Holy Year?

No change has been announced. You still need 100 km on foot or horseback, or 200 km by bike, with a stamped credential. The wait may be longer.

### Do I have to walk to go through the Holy Door?

No. The cathedral says the jubilee can be gained travelling by any means, and it is separate from the Compostela.

### What is the quietest time to walk in 2027?

January, February and December, then November. Expect short days, rain and some closed albergues.

### Can I reserve a bed in a public albergue?

Not in the Xunta's network. Most private albergues take bookings.

### Can I cycle from Sarria and get a Compostela?

No. Cyclists need 200 km. Ponferrada (about 204 km by Gronze's stages) or Porto clears it.

### When is the next Holy Year after 2027?

2032, the next time 25 July falls on a Sunday.
